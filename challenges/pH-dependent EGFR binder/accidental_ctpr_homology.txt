HOW WE FOUND OUT, BY ACCIDENT, THAT OUR DE NOVO BRIDGE DESIGNS ARE
CONSENSUS TETRATRICOPEPTIDE REPEAT PROTEINS

James McCann and the Koder Group, The City College of New York
Lab note, 2026-10-06 (NYC local). Measured on a local CPU.


WHAT WE WERE DOING

For challenge 1 we designed de novo binders to the human EGFR extracellular
region. Most of them are 75-80 aa single-site binders. Three of them -- we call
them "bridges", ids 85, 1104 and 47 -- are 145 aa and were built to span two
separate epitopes on the receptor at once. They came out of the ordinary
pipeline: a backbone generator run against the receptor surface, then a neural
sequence designer (SolubleMPNN) on the resulting backbone, then structure
prediction to check the fold. No existing protein was used as a template, no
sequence was copied, and no homolog of anything was searched for at design
time. We did not know what they resembled because we never asked.

Before submitting we ran the novelty check that the competition's annotation
pipeline runs, because we wanted to know what label our own designs would come
back with. That is the only reason we looked. The
homology below is what the check returned.


WHAT WE FOUND

All three bridges are close structural and sequence relatives of the published
consensus tetratricopeptide repeat (CTPR) proteins.

  structure   TM-score 0.967-0.977 to the nearest known structure, over
              0.77-0.81 of the design (foldseek, local PDB structure set)
  sequence    59.0-64.2% local identity (foldseek, aligned region, query
              coverage >= 0.50); 41-46% identity over the full query by
              MMseqs2 against Swiss-Prot and PDB sequences

The nearest neighbours, by both halves, are the CTPR series: 7OBI ("Consensus
tetratricopeptide repeat protein type RV4"), 2AVP ("Crystal structure of an 8
repeat consensus TPR superhelix"), 1NA0/1NA3 ("Design of Stable alpha-Helical
Arrays from an Idealized TPR Motif"), 2WQH ("CTPR3Y3"), 3KD7 ("Designed TPR
module (CTPR390)...") and 2HYZ. Titles are verbatim from the PDB entries. The
single strongest sequence hit is id85 against 1na0_A at E = 4.4e-33.

The point worth recording is what those neighbours are. They are not natural
proteins that our designs happen to echo. They are themselves designed
proteins -- their own PDB titles say "consensus", "designed" and "idealized" --
built as biophysical model systems rather than as binders. 7OBI's primary
citation is Synakewicz et al., ACS Nano 2022, on the mechanics of
repeat-protein nanosprings. So the nearest neighbour of our de novo design is
somebody else's de novo design. (One natural protein does appear in the list:
5A01, the TPR superhelix domain of O-GlcNAc transferase.)


WHY WE THINK IT HAPPENED -- A HYPOTHESIS, NOT A RESULT

The bridges contain an internal repeat. A self-identity scan over periods of
20-60 aa finds the best period at 34 residues at 82-85% self-identity; 34
residues is exactly the TPR period. Split at 34, the two internal repeats are
76-82% identical to each other, while the N- and C-terminal caps are already
divergent (9% to each other). The canonical CTPR consensus is
AEAWYNLGNAYYKQGDYDEAIEYYQKALELDP; our internal repeats carry
A.AGKYEEAIEYY.KAIELDPNN, and the AIEYY.KA.ELDP core is near-identical.

Our reading -- and we state it as a hypothesis, because we have not tested it
-- is that the consensus TPR sequence is close to the optimum a sequence
designer finds for a TPR-like backbone. Given a repeat backbone, the designer
appears to have converged on the published consensus independently rather than
retrieved it from anywhere. That is consistent with why the CTPR series exists
at all: consensus design is used precisely because the consensus is the
stability optimum of that fold.

We have not shown this. An alternative we cannot exclude from here is that the
backbone generator itself produced a TPR-like backbone because TPRs are well
represented in its training data, in which case the convergence happens one
stage earlier than we are describing.


THE FOLLOW-UP, WHICH IS THE PART WE THINK IS USEFUL TO OTHERS

We then tried to move the sequence off the consensus without touching the
binding surface. Using the same sequence designer with the reference residue
explicitly forbidden at each chosen position, we redesigned 35-44 non-interface
positions per bridge and refolded the mutants and their parents under identical
settings, 3 seeds x 21 diffusion samples = 63 draws per molecule.

  sequence identity   fell from 59-64% to 19-31%
  interface residues  zero changed
  fold                delta TM = +0.002 / -0.005 / -0.030
                      all 189 mutant draws still at or above TM 0.80

The sequence half moved by 30-45 points and the structural half did not move at
all. Sequence novelty and structural novelty are separable properties here, and
changing one of them did not change the other. We asked the designer to change
the sequence while keeping the fold, and it did exactly that.

This is a negative result for what we were trying to achieve -- the novelty
classification is unchanged, because high structural similarity is on its own
sufficient to cap it -- and it is a clean positive measurement of the
separability. Anyone trying to make a repeat-scaffold design "more novel" by
diversifying its sequence should expect the same outcome.


SCOPE, AND WHAT THIS IS NOT

- This affects the three 145 aa bridges only. The nine 75-80 aa single-site
  designs have no internal repeat (best period 21 aa at 23.8% self-identity)
  and returned zero statistically significant hits in Swiss-Prot or PDB
  sequences by either of two independent search tools.

- Nothing here is a binding measurement. No design in this repository has been
  tested at the bench, and no number anywhere in it is an affinity.

- Our searches covered the local PDB structure set, Swiss-Prot (release
  2026_03, 575,748 sequences) and PDB sequences (1,103,516 protein chains). We
  did not search patent sequences or antibody-specific databases. Additional
  databases can only raise the maximum identity found, never lower it, so every
  similarity figure above is a lower bound and every novelty figure an upper
  bound.

- We make no claim about what the competition's annotation will report. Its
  identity convention, E-value threshold and domain-segmentation method are not
  known to us, and on our own numbers the convention alone is worth up to about
  15 percentage points.


WHY WE ARE WRITING IT DOWN

We found this while doing due diligence on our own submission, not while
looking for homologs, and it would have been easy not to find at all: nothing
in the design or the fold check flags it, and the designs look novel right up
until somebody searches. Anyone running a backbone generator plus a neural
sequence designer on a repeat-like or symmetric backbone can land on a
published consensus sequence without any step of their pipeline reading that
sequence. It is worth searching before submitting, publishing or filing, and it
is worth searching the de novo design literature specifically -- our designs
are novel against nature and markedly less novel against other people's
designs. As structure databases keep accumulating deposited designed proteins,
that margin should be expected to erode.
