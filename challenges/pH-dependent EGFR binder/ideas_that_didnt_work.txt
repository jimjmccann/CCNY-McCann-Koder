IDEAS THAT DIDN'T WORK
Things we tried on the de novo EGFR binder campaign (2026-09-28 .. 2026-10-06)
that did not work, and why.
McCann / Koder groups, The City College of New York.

Ordered roughly by how much they cost us. Every entry has a measurement behind
it; where it does not, the entry says so. Several entries are things that worked
mechanically and were retracted on their MEANING — those are the expensive ones,
because they read exactly like the true results.

A note on the record: several entries below correct or retract earlier entries
of our own. Those retractions are kept, not tidied away. A retraction is a
finding.


=====================================================================
1. A CONTIGUOUS CROP OF THE TARGET. Voided an entire round.
=====================================================================
TRIED: generate and fold binders against a 106-residue window of the receptor
(and a 77-residue window for the second site) instead of the whole thing, because
the generator needs a smaller target than 621 residues.

WHAT HAPPENED: the window was validated as "all 11 footprint residues are
present" and never as "nothing important is absent". Measured afterwards:

  site A  kept WT 370-475 (106 aa), omitted 204 residues
          70% of designs within 2 A of omitted receptor (median 1.14 A, n=300)
  site B  kept WT 4-80 (77 aa), omitted 527 residues
          100% of designs within 2 A of omitted receptor (median 0.34 A, n=300)

Worked example, the best-scoring site-A model of 48,981: receptor residue 346's
BACKBONE sat 0.66-1.01 A from binder heavy atoms (N 1.01, CA 0.66, C 0.98,
O 0.74, CB 0.93). Backbone, not side chain — no rotamer relieves it.
Superposition was 0.52 A over all 106 shared CA, so this is not a fitting
artifact. Residue 346 was in the file the crop was cut FROM, the whole time.

Not moot: the assay construct is the complete 621-residue ectodomain, read out of
the organiser's own data, and position 346 is present in it.

HOW IT GOT THROUGH, three compounding causes:
  - the crop was CHANGED (WT 310-416 -> 370-475) to recover three footprint
    residues at one end. It was a SHIFT, not an extension, and silently deleted
    WT 310-369 at the other end. The old crop contained residue 346.
  - the omission WAS noticed two days later and filed under a heading about
    numbering being verified. The tick went to the arithmetic; the residue's
    absence was the evidence rather than the subject.
  - no script in the pipeline could detect it. Every downstream stage treated
    the crop AS the target, so the omission was unfalsifiable from inside.

LESSON: validate a truncated target by EXCLUSION, not inclusion. A check that
cannot fail is not a check. We now superpose each design back onto the whole
receptor and measure overlap with what the generation target did not contain.
That gate is the single most publishable thing in this project.

STATUS: everything from the generation stage onward was VOIDED and quarantined.
It may be read for METHOD questions only, with every number marked, and never
plotted on the same axes as later data.


=====================================================================
2. INHERITING THE SAMPLE COUNT FROM ANOTHER CAMPAIGN. A whole arm for nothing.
=====================================================================
TRIED: 21 structure-prediction draws per sequence, carried over from an unrelated
campaign where a pose statistic needed 21.

WHAT HAPPENED: 21 draws had succeeded only on small crops (127-176 tokens). On
the full 696-token construct with 10 glycans, glycan bond constraints, a forced
template and potentials enabled, it had never been tried. Six workers launched;
FIVE PRODUCED NOTHING in ~9.7 h on a 71-minute job. The sixth landed only because
the container's restart loop gave it a second attempt.

Failure signature, from the one shard that kept a log:
  "WARNING: ran out of memory, skipping batch"  x4
  "Number of failed examples: 4"
  "boltz rc=0  predictions 1/5"

Two traps inside the failure:
  - rc=0. The predictor catches its own OOM, skips the batch, writes no
    structure, returns success.
  - non-deterministic. The re-run reported zero failures. One worker of six
    surviving is NOT evidence the configuration works.
  - the worker's own fatal-error file blamed a missing MSA. The MSA was present.

Also recorded: this was flagged IN ADVANCE in the project log ("choose it
deliberately, do not inherit it") and inherited anyway.

LESSON: the sample count is a function of construct size. Justify it per campaign
and canary an unmeasured count on one shard first.

STATUS: 3 is the only count ever proven on that construct; 10 was chosen as an
interpolation and then separately proven (0 failures over 1,040 structures).


=====================================================================
3. A SURROGATE MODEL IN THE OPTIMIZATION LOOP. Measured out, twice.
=====================================================================
TRIED: the standard active-learning shape — fit a model to the landed cycle, rank
candidates with upper-confidence-bound acquisition, fold the top ones.

WHAT HAPPENED, site A: out-of-bag RMSE / sd(y) was 1.14 on one label and 1.04 on
the other. Both >= 1.0, i.e. at or worse than predicting the mean. Trained on the
saturated label it predicted 11.696 and 12.101 against a physical ceiling of 11.
UCB did not beat random: +0.394 +/- 0.634, t=+0.62, Fisher p=1.000.

WHAT HAPPENED, the two-site bridge arm: the gate failed at n=50 (ratio 1.056),
failed at n=60 (1.028 +/- 0.019, Spearman +0.136, AUROC 0.579). We then measured
the learning curve by subsampling, 3 repeats per size:

  n=20  1.077 +/- 0.080     n=50  1.077 +/- 0.024
  n=30  1.031 +/- 0.122     n=60  1.028
  n=40  1.064 +/- 0.133

FLAT across a 3x range, and n=60 sat inside n=30's scatter. The apparent gain
from pooling an earlier campaign's sequences was noise.

OUR OWN CORRECTION: an earlier conclusion that "n is the constraint" is
WITHDRAWN by that curve. n is not the binding constraint either — a 3-draw label
is not learnable from sequence at any n we can reach on one backbone. The fix is
more DRAWS per sequence, not more sequences. A large part of the label is simply
whether the predictor folds the design at all (sc_rmsd rho -0.506), and
predicting that from sequence is the hard part.

LESSON: a surrogate must be shown to beat predicting the mean before it is
allowed to rank anything, and the gate must be re-run every cycle. State it with
its standard error: a ratio of 0.999 once printed as "1.00" and was read as a
pass. Require ratio + 1 s.e. < 1.0.

STATUS: no model in either loop. Site A ran exhaustive enumeration instead, which
needs no ranking, so nothing was lost. The bridge is a model-free screen with
replication.

4. RANKING BACKBONES ON INTERFACE CONTACT ALONE. Four of six picks were
   fold artifacts.
=====================================================================
TRIED: rank candidate backbones by how many footprint residues their designs
contact. It is the quantity we actually want.

WHAT HAPPENED: a first version of that ranking named six backbones. FOUR OF THE
SIX FAIL THE FOLD GATE. Measured, after self-consistency was run over 3,246
models:

  backbone (contact-only rank)   median sc_rmsd   gate   seqs pass   full-A kept
  #1  arm_shard09_..._model_7         8.45 A      FAIL      0/9        11 -> 0
      arm_shard05_..._model_4         3.06 A      FAIL      0/9        12 -> 0
      arm_shard07_..._model_3         1.80 A      FAIL      1/10       14 -> 3
      arm_shard13_..._model_0         1.38 A      pass      6/9         5 -> 2
      arm_shard10_..._model_4         0.98 A      pass      7/9        11 -> 7
      arm_shard00_..._model_0         0.64 A      pass      9/9        10 -> 10

The contact-only leader had a median Ca fold error of 8.45 A. The predictor did
not build that backbone's designed shape, so every one of its 11
"full-footprint" draws was contact against a different structure.

Arm-wide: the fold gate removes 53% of full-footprint contact, 422 draws -> 200.
31 of 118 backbones fail the median gate, and 16 of those 31 had >=5
full-footprint draws — the contact metric was actively PROMOTING unbuildable
backbones.

LESSON: gate on fold quality first, then rank on contacts. Keep fold fidelity and
placement fidelity as two separate numbers.

STATUS: the contact-only list is RETRACTED in its own document, with the
retracted table left in place so the comparison stays visible. The funnel now
runs designability first.

5. "OUR EPITOPE IS THE RIGHT ONE" FROM A SINGLE-CROP AUROC. Retracted by
   its own pre-registered control.
=====================================================================
TRIED: fold 402 prior competition designs with published binding outcomes against
our chosen face, measure how well footprint engagement predicts measured binding.
AUROC 0.633, CI excluding 0.5, p=0.0055 — and it beat all 21 confidence metrics
we had.

WHAT HAPPENED: every design had been folded against our face ALONE, so a design
whose real epitope is elsewhere had nowhere else to dock. The control — the same
402 designs folded against a face almost nobody had targeted — scored HIGHER.

  matched 248-design cohort, one script, one bootstrap recipe:
    our site     0.633 [0.546, 0.717]  p=0.0055
    control      0.656 [0.576, 0.730]  p=0.0012
    paired A-B  -0.022, 95% CI [-0.135, +0.094]

Indistinguishable, with the point estimate favouring the control.

THEN THE CORRECTION WAS ITSELF CORRECTED. Stratifying revealed the pool is a
MIXTURE: 99 cysteine-rich ligand-mimic designs (binding rate 29.3%) and 149
genuinely designed interfaces (11.4%). The mimics engage the control face MORE
and our face LESS, so pooling inflated the control and suppressed ours —
Simpson's paradox. In the designed stratum:

                     our site | length        control | length
    designed n=149   0.715 [0.582,0.827] p=0.0045    0.557 [0.400,0.707] p=0.45

Our site survives a length control among designed interfaces; the control face
does not. Dose-response across quartiles in that stratum: 2.7% -> 5.4% -> 15.8%
-> 21.6%, an 8x spread.

BUT THE DIRECT CONTRAST STILL FAILS: paired difference within the designed
stratum = +0.087, 95% CI [-0.097, +0.264]. "A passes its own test and B fails its
own" is WEAKER than "A beats B". Two underpowered tests with opposite verdicts
are not a measured difference, and 17 binders cannot make them one.

LESSONS, three:
  - any epitope-quality claim ships with a decoy arm, and the retracting
    threshold is written down before the number is computed. That is why this was
    a two-hour correction instead of a premise in the next three documents.
  - quote that class of metric STRATIFIED or not at all. Quoting the pooled value
    is quoting the paradox.
  - the metric is kept under the weaker name "docks tightly to the face it is
    handed". It still ranks. It sets no threshold.

STATUS: the epitope-specific claim is RETRACTED, with a banner in the source
document's own bytes that is never to be removed. The AUROC itself stands and
reproduces byte-for-byte; what was withdrawn is what it MEANT. The site choice
rests on its original structural rationale, which this never supported.


=====================================================================
6. OPTIMISING ENGAGEMENT AS A DESIGN OBJECTIVE. Blocked before it ran.
=====================================================================
TRIED (proposed, not run): since footprint engagement predicts measured binding,
make it the thing we optimise.

WHY IT WAS BLOCKED: measured, binder LENGTH alone scores AUROC 0.575 and
correlates with engagement (+0.249 at our site, +0.379 at the control). Length is
a variable we set at design time. Optimising engagement directly would partly
optimise "make it longer" — Goodhart, not affinity. The amendment made it worse,
not better: length is an INDEPENDENT predictor in the designed stratum (0.700,
p=0.0055 raw; 0.676, p=0.0157 after regressing out engagement).

LESSON: never optimise a metric that correlates with a variable you choose.
Regress the design-time variable out first, or keep the metric as a rank.

RELATED, same failure class, from another project in this lab: a design loss that
optimised POSE and contained no affinity term at all, and a confidence term that
turned out to be a reward channel for low-confidence poses (non-zero only in the
least-confident 0.3% of designs) and was dropped.


=====================================================================
7. GATE ON ONE SITE, GRADE ON THE OTHER. Selects on the outcome.
=====================================================================
TRIED, for the two-site bridge: keep only draws that fold AND reach >=9 of 11
residues at site A, then score the mean site-B contact over those draws. Plus a
">=50% of draws must qualify" floor.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured on 50 sequences x 3 draws:
  - the two sites are correlated, +0.590 (p=1.9e-15) over 150 fold-passing
    draws. Mean site-B contact is 4.15 when site A >= 9 and 2.81 when it is not.
    So dropping non-qualifying draws preferentially deletes the LOW site-B
    measurements. The label became an average over a sample chosen by the thing
    being averaged.
  - the inflation lands exactly where it does damage. One sequence scored 7.00
    from a single qualifying draw while its three draws were [7, 6, 0] — true
    mean 4.33, inflation +2.67. 10 of 36 labels rested on one draw; 4 of the top
    10. Kendall tau between the biased and fixed rules is +0.714 and that
    sequence falls from 1st to outside the top 5.
  - the gate discarded data it should have scored: 14 of 50 sequences got no
    label at all, including one that reached site B 7/7 in a draw.

LESSON: scoring zero is different from not reporting a score. A misfolded or
mispositioned draw is still a data point. The only admissible missing value is a
draw where the predictor produced no structure at all — which here never
happened (1200/1200 structures, 10/10 manifests rc=0). Every exclusion we had
made was discarding a real measurement.

STATUS: replaced by a per-draw floor, min(site A / 11, site B / 7), zero if the
draw misfolds, averaged over EVERY draw. The ">=50%" floor became redundant: the
12 sequences it would have rejected score exactly 0.00 anyway. Gate, floor and
label collapsed into one honest number.


=====================================================================
8. COUNTING THE DRAWS THAT SATISFY EVERYTHING. Arithmetically impossible
   at 3 draws.
=====================================================================
TRIED: the cleanest statement of the objective — count, per sequence, how many
draws satisfy both sites completely.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured on 50 sequences at 3 draws:

  candidate objective                      usable seqs  distinct values  zeros
  gate-and-grade (entry 7)                     36            17           -
  mean site-B over folded draws                50            20           -
  count of strict both-site draws              50             1          50
  count, relaxed                               50             4          28
  per-draw min floor (adopted)                 50            33           7

A count over 3 draws can take at most 4 values. That is a hard ceiling on
resolution, not a tuning problem.

LESSON: check the resolution arithmetic of a candidate objective on real data —
distinct values, zeros, median, max — before adopting it. A metric with fewer
distinct values than you have sequences cannot rank them.

STATUS: the strict criterion is WATCHED as its own column and never regressed on.
It is non-zero on exactly one backbone.


=====================================================================
9. AVERAGING EACH REQUIREMENT SEPARATELY. Rewards designs that never once
   do the thing we want.
=====================================================================
TRIED: score each requirement (footprint coverage, the key residue, the second
site) as its own average and combine the averages.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured on real draws:
  - one design held every one of the 11 footprint residues in >=50% of its draws
    and covered all 11 together in 1 draw in 10.
  - on the bridge, one sequence scored 0.857 with each site averaged separately
    — near the top of the table — and had ZERO both-site draws. The per-draw min
    gives it 0.571.
  - Kendall tau between the two rankings is +0.783. This changes the ORDER, not
    just the scale.

LESSON: marginal coverage overstates same-draw coverage. Average the per-draw
conjunction; never conjoin the per-draw averages. Never a sum — a near-best term
cancels a near-worst one.

RELATED EXPECTATION THAT WAS ALSO WRONG, in the other direction: the expectation
going in was that a HARD requirement would zero ~36% of draws and recreate the
resolution collapse of entry 8. Measured over 98 sequences at 10 draws, the hard
floor gave 31 distinct values and NO zeros at all, against the old metric's 11 —
roughly 3x the resolution. The first recommendation was reversed by the
measurement. Scope matters though: that no-zeros property depends on averaging
over enough draws, and at 3 draws the same shape of metric leaves 21 of 50
sequences at exactly 0.


=====================================================================
10. BOTH OF OUR RANKING METRICS. Saturated.
=====================================================================
TRIED: rank designs on mean footprint contact per draw, then on the fraction of
draws achieving full footprint contact.

WHAT HAPPENED: the first metric has a hard ceiling of 11 — the footprint size.
By cycle 1 the top three sat at IDENTICAL means (dmean +0.00, Fisher
p=0.628-1.000). The fallback then also hit its ceiling of 1.000 on two designs,
which both passed the fold gate 10/10 and were not separable by anything we had.
A single design's fraction carries standard error ~0.16 at 10 draws, so
1.000 vs 1.000 vs 0.900 is inside the noise, and draws could not be raised to fix
it (97.6% GPU memory already, and OOM exits rc=0).

LESSON: saturation is foreseeable from the definition. Check for a ceiling before
building the loop. When a metric saturates, only REPLICATION separates the
leaders — a new metric does not, if it reduces to the same thing at the top.

MEASURED CONFIRMATION OF THAT LAST POINT: the replacement objective, at the top
of the table, reduces EXACTLY to the key-residue contact rate (63 of 98
sequences, and all ten re-measured top designs). It improves resolution in the
middle — 53 distinct values vs 14 over 142 designs / 1,520 draws, Kendall tau
0.707, and it moved one design from rank 63 to rank 7 — but it does NOT
unsaturate the leaders.


=====================================================================
11. THE COMBINATION WALK. Enumerated out, beat nothing.
=====================================================================
TRIED: find the single mutations that beat the reference on both metrics (the
"accepted set"), then enumerate untried combinations of them, lowest order first.

WHAT HAPPENED: cycles 1-3 enumerated all 88 combinations (50 at orders 2-3, 38 at
orders 4-6; no order 7 exists). The driver's own line on the last of them:
"cycle sequences beating the previous best: 0". Enumeration is now exhausted, so
the proposer has no derivable content and the next cycle is a science decision,
not a scheduling one.

WHY, MEASURED: the accepted set contained NO position that reaches the
rate-limiting contact (see entry 12). The walk was optimising the eight residues
it could already reach.

LESSON: enumeration ends, and an exhausted enumeration is a finding about the
reference, not about the method. Know what your accepted set can and cannot
reach before you spend three cycles on it.

12. POSITION IDENTITY AS THE LEVER ON THE LIMITING CONTACT. A clean null.
=====================================================================
BACKGROUND, measured: one footprint residue is contacted in 63.8% of 600 draws
and 63.7% of a later 380 fresh draws — the weakest of the 11 by ~13 points
(the strongest is 98.8%). Of draws missing the ceiling by exactly ONE residue, it
is that residue in 89.8% and 81.9% of cases. So it is the RATE-LIMITING contact.
The rate did not move when the mutation order went 1-3 -> 4-6, which makes it
ARCHITECTURAL rather than a property of the substitutions explored. One binder
position is effectively the only handle on it: within 5 A of it in 248 of the 310
draws that contact it (80%); the next position is second at 7.7%.

Causal evidence that the position governs it: substituting it to glycine takes
the score 0.40 -> 0.00 while FOLDING BETTER than the parent (median fold 0.587 vs
0.612, gate 10/10, floor PASS). 18 of 19 substitutions there are unfolded.

TRIED: a saturation scan at that position — 18 substitutions plus 6 replicates,
24 folds, 240 structures.

WHAT HAPPENED: NO substitution beats the champions. Best scan design 0.609
against the leading champion's 1.000. Against its OWN parent, 0.609 vs 0.600 —
inside the 0.158 noise floor measured in the same cycle, so NOT a difference.

  rank  seq      new metric   old metric   fold    note
    1   id2007     1.000        1.00       10/10   replicate of a champion
    3   id2015     0.800        0.80       10/10   replicate of a champion
    6   id4004     0.609        0.40       10/10   BEST SCAN DESIGN
   11   id41       0.600        0.60       10/10   the scan's own parent

LESSON: distinguish a residue that is NECESSARY from one that is RATE-LIMITING,
and then distinguish a position that GOVERNS a quantity from one that can IMPROVE
it. All three are different claims. The identity at that position was not the
lever; the pose was.

Also note id4004's metric split — 0.609 on the new objective, 0.400 on the old,
rank 6 vs rank 12. That is the tau 0.707 disagreement doing real work on the one
design a reader would care about. Label the metric.

WHAT IT WAS WORTH: this was launched as a MECHANISM cycle, chosen deliberately
over a submission cycle, and the lower scores were PREDICTED IN WRITING before
launch (the parent's own score is 0.400, so its descendants score lower by
construction). A predicted null is not a regression.

13. CLASSIFIER-FREE GUIDANCE AND EXTRA RECYCLING ON THE GENERATOR. Refuted.
=====================================================================
TRIED: turn on classifier-free guidance (scale 1.5) because the authors had built
it specifically for the conditioning channels our design depends on, and left it
off by default. And raise the recycle count.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured against a null: guidance 2.76x the null. Recycling 2.55x
null-to-worse. Both expensive sampler knobs were turned OFF for production.

LESSON: an argument from "the authors built this for exactly our case" is a reason
to TEST a knob, not to ship it. Both of these had good stories.

WORTH KEEPING: the test that refuted the generator choice itself was specified in
advance as a 32-minute, 3-arm run with the gate written down — if the conditioned
arm matched the control arm, the whole generator was to be dropped for an
alternative. Decisions built to be refutable in 32 minutes are cheap to hold.


=====================================================================
14. CONDITIONING THE GENERATOR ON BURIAL / EXPOSURE. Not expressible.
=====================================================================
TRIED: tell the generator to keep a titratable residue at the rim of the
interface rather than buried — the natural way to express a pH-switch
requirement.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured by reading the installed package rather than the docs:
the burial/exposure channels have ~0% training share for the protein-only case,
and the channel dies in a transform that early-returns when there is no ligand,
on BOTH pipeline branches. The instruction is not sayable at all.

More generally: the generator can be told WHAT TO TOUCH and cannot be told WHAT
TO AVOID. The previous version exposed an anti-hotspot parameter; that capability
is gone. Every "avoid" requirement therefore lives in the filter.

A RELATED CLAIM OF OURS THAT WAS ALSO WRONG: an earlier note said an external
hydrogen-bond program was REQUIRED by the pipeline. It is mapped to an identity
operation at inference. Believing it had suppressed the one channel that
expresses the switch.

LESSON: read the trained capability in the source before planning a campaign
around it. Generate wide, filter hard, and recognise that the filter is where the
design actually happens — it is not QC.


=====================================================================
15. A SPREAD HOTSPOT SET. Unsatisfiable, and we measured why.
=====================================================================
TRIED: ask the generator for all six hotspots across a site.

WHAT HAPPENED, measured: orientation is inferred from the hotspot CENTROID. One
requested hotspot 12.2 A from the centroid of the others was reached by 0 of 336
designs, and the correlation between a hotspot's distance from the centroid and
its contact rate was +0.967.

LESSON: a spread constraint set may be geometrically unsatisfiable. Run the
"everything" arm and a "minimal anchors only" arm together; if the first returns
nothing and the second returns designs, the spread is the problem and you learned
it for one shard instead of a campaign. We did exactly that for the two-site
bridge after this.


=====================================================================
16. IMPORTING A SPHERICITY SCREEN FROM ANOTHER PROJECT. Wrong metric for
    the problem.
=====================================================================
TRIED: reuse an existing shape-triage screen that ranks designs by sphericity.

WHY IT IS WRONG: that screen was built for a project needing a GLOBULAR protein
to hold a cofactor in an interior. A binder has the opposite requirement — it
must present a complementary face to a flat epitope. A highly spherical binder is
if anything worse: it minimises contact area per residue.

AND THE REST OF THAT TOOLING DID NOT TRANSFER EITHER, checked: one component was
a control experiment that states it pre-registers no gate; another is descriptive
(secondary-structure composition and novelty) rather than a thresholded filter;
and it depends on a system library that is broken on this machine. There was no
ready-made filter to reuse at all.

LESSON: ask what a borrowed metric was FOR before reusing it. "We already have a
screen" is not a reason.


=====================================================================
17. PERMITTING CYSTEINE AND OMITTING PROLINE. The inverse error, live in
    this same repository.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENED on an earlier arm of this lab: the sequence step permitted cysteine
and excluded proline. Measured consequence: 2-3 cysteines per design in a protein
whose wild type has ZERO, and they NEVER form a disulfide — partners 12-15 A
apart. Those are free thiols: an oxidation and aggregation liability with no
compensating structure. It confounded the design pool, and the question had to be
deferred by a formal decision rather than resolved, because the campaign was
already running on them.

Omitting proline is the second half of the same mistake where caps and turns are
load-bearing: free backbone NH and C=O exist only at helix caps and in loops —
mid-helix amides are consumed by the helix's own i->i+4 bonds — and proline is
the capping residue. Omitting it forces caps out of residues that cap badly.

LESSON: both settings existed in this repository at the same time and only one was
right for a single-chain binder. Audit your own flags per campaign. Cysteine out,
proline in, plus an N-glycosylation sequon grep, is a free filter and is routine
practice at frontier labs.


=====================================================================
18. THE ACIDIC SEQUENCE BIAS. Decided, then measured to zero.
=====================================================================
TRIED, in two steps. First: let the generator's own sequence head write
sequences, then filter for the acidic net charge the brief requires. Measured on
8 smoke designs, 3 of 8 passed and ALL FIVE failures were on the acidity bound —
the sequence head writes near-neutral binders. At the handful of designs
ultimately tested experimentally, discarding ~62% of the pool for a property we
could request is unaffordable. So the decision was to BIAS IT IN at the sequence
step, with the magnitude deliberately left to a tuning batch.

WHAT HAPPENED WHEN THE BATCH WAS MEASURED, n = 3,320 sequences
(332 backbones x 10, both sites):

              NCPR median   in band    too POSITIVE   too NEGATIVE
  site A         -0.100     77.6%           44            283
  site B        -0.0909     74.6%          138            335

⛔ THE POOL ALREADY OVERSHOOTS ON ACIDITY ABOUT 6:1. A D/E bias would have pushed
MORE sequences out the BOTTOM of the band. The n=8 smoke test was wrong about the
DIRECTION, not just the size.

⇒ THE BIAS SHIPPED AT ZERO. The rest of the sequence-step decision stood:
cysteine omitted (measured cysteine-free 3,320 / 3,320), proline permitted.

LESSONS, two, and the second is the bigger one:
  (a) measure-then-commit earned its keep — a magnitude set from n=8 would have
      hurt, and the only reason it did not is that the decision refused to fix
      the number;
  (b) a decision can be RIGHT about which property to control and WRONG about
      which way. An n=8 pilot can establish that a property needs attention; it
      cannot establish the sign.

ALSO LEARNED HERE: the filter step SPLITS, because its inputs do. Net charge and
surface-patch metrics need a SEQUENCE, so they cannot run on backbones at all.

AND THE SEQUON FILTER WAS NOT FREE, measured on the same pass: 322 sequences
(9.7%) carried an N-X-[ST] sequon IN THE BINDER and were filtered out. Combined
with the charge band and the cysteine check, the fold set came to 2,260 sequences
over 317 of 332 backbones — the filters cost 15 backbones, not 15%.


=====================================================================
19. A CONSTANT RESIDUE-NUMBER OFFSET. Three scorers read the wrong residue.
=====================================================================
TRIED: map our numbering to the generator's output with a constant offset. Valid
for a contiguous window, and it was correct for one.

WHAT HAPPENED: the replacement sphere cut is MULTI-SEGMENT — 15 segments at one
site, 26 at the other — and the generator renumbers the target 1..N contiguous,
discarding both the original numbers and every gap. No constant offset exists.
Three scorers had one hardcoded. Measured: the key histidine is output residue
B141; the offset formula gives B40, which is a CYSTEINE. One site failed loudly
on a residue-type assertion. The other site's offset "worked" by luck, because
all eight of its footprint residues fell in the sphere's FIRST segment.

Worse, nothing had persisted the real map: the builder computed it and threw it
away, so it was recoverable only by re-running the cut at the identical radius.

A SECOND, RELATED FAILURE: a guidepost-excision step took the binder's nearest
aspartate to the key histidine and REMOVED it from the binder before measuring
footprint contact. On hotspot-only arms there is no guidepost, so that deleted a
real, designed residue — and it fired precisely on the designs that place an
aspartate at the imidazole, i.e. on the interaction the arm existed to make. It
was deleted, not bypassed.

LESSON: persist the map at cut time, ship it with the target, have every consumer
load it, and verify it against real generator output by RESIDUE TYPE (231/231 and
230/230 here), not by count. An anchor-only check cannot catch a wrong map. And
assert residue identity rather than a number range: the design target is numbered
1..231 and the assay construct 1..621, so the integer overlap is a coincidence.


=====================================================================
20. "RELAX THE SCORER'S CHECK SO IT ACCEPTS THE NEW FOLDS."
=====================================================================
TRIED (proposed, not done): the existing self-consistency scorer refused the new
folds with "target residues differ: design 231 vs pred 621 (shared 231)". The
obvious move is to loosen the count check.

WHY IT WAS NOT DONE: the scorer was RIGHT to refuse. Design residue 1 is WT 8;
fold residue 1 is WT 1. Relaxing the check would have superposed MISMATCHED
residues and returned a confident, meaningless placement number. A second scorer
was written instead, routing the target leg through the persisted map and
asserting identity after mapping.

LESSON: a tool that refuses your input may be right. Read the refusal before
removing it.


=====================================================================
21. THE TRAINING-TABLE MERGE PATH. Never worked at all.
=====================================================================
TRIED: a merge script to fold each cycle's results into the training table.

WHAT HAPPENED: it POOLS sequences already present in the base table and SKIPS new
ones — "is new but has NO SEQUENCE in the base table" — which is every sequence in
a mutant cycle. So the first real cycle had no working merge path. Cycle 0's rows
had been built by an inline heredoc that was not kept.

LESSON: a path that is never exercised on the real case is not a path. This is
why the cycle scorer exists as a file.

STATUS: dead end, excluded from everything. Not published.


=====================================================================
22. TREATING REPEATS AS NEW DATA. Would have corrupted the table.
=====================================================================
WHAT WOULD HAVE HAPPENED: a repeat is a duplicate SEQUENCE. Merging it into the
training table puts one sequence in twice, double-weights it in anything fitted
on the table, corrupts the reference detection (which counts Hamming-1
neighbours), and lets a repeat become the parent of the next cycle.

AND THE OBVIOUS SHORTCUT IS ALSO WRONG: identifying repeats by id prefix. "id9"
as a prefix already matched ten genuine designs (id9, id90, id91..id99, id901,
id902), so prefix matching would have held out real data. Repeats are identified
from their own declared file only; if that file is absent the scorer behaves
exactly as it did before repeats existed, which is what makes the earlier cycles
reproduce byte-for-byte.

LESSON: declare repeats in a file, never infer them from a name.


=====================================================================
23. ASSUMING THE CAMPAIGN HAD REPLICATES BECAUSE SOME ROWS LOOKED LIKE THEM.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENED: two rows carried a doubled draw count and looked like replicates.
Measured: they are BYTE-IDENTICAL across three successive training tables —
carried forward, never re-measured. Every design in the campaign had exactly ONE
measurement, through four cycles, so there was no noise estimate at all.

The concept had been raised twice and never became a plan. One report warned in
writing that two byte-identical draw vectors must not be read "as two independent
confirmations" — a caution about the absence, not a remedy. An earlier decision
left a replicate-agreement criterion explicitly open, with its motivating
failure: a control whose between-replicate spread was 0.33 kcal/mol against a
per-replicate error of ~0.06 — replicas disagreeing ~5x more than their own
statistics permitted, invisible while a single replicate was quoted. A third
document recorded a standing unfixed objection that our submission set contains
no replicates at all, against measured assay replicate scatter of 2.8x at the
90th percentile.

LESSON: "we have replicates" is a claim about the filesystem. Check it. A warning
written down three times is not a plan.

STATUS: replication is now standing — top ~5 per cycle refolded with fresh draws,
riding in the cycle's own slots at zero extra cost, with a known LOW anchor
included so regression to the mean is measurable. First in-cycle run: n=6, mean
|delta| 0.158, slope of fresh-on-prior +0.343. Both champions at 1.000 came back
0.700 and 0.800; the low anchor came UP 0.400 -> 0.600. Part of every cycle's
apparent gain was SELECTION.


=====================================================================
24. ANCHORING ON THE TOP OF THE TABLE. Flattered everything downstream.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENED: one design topped the table at 10.333 on a gate of 3 passing draws
in 10, with a median fold error of 1.871 A — it fails the fold gate 7 draws in
10. Anchoring the next cycle on a thin-data outlier redefines every mutation in
that cycle relative to a design that does not fold.

LESSON: apply every quality floor to the REFERENCE as well as to the candidates.
And apply the noise floor to the BASELINE too: the honest baseline here is the
hub design at 10.05 on 20 draws, not its single best reading.


=====================================================================
25. SEQUENCE-MODEL CONFIDENCE AS A WITHIN-BACKBONE RANKER.
=====================================================================
TRIED: pick which 50 of 90 sequences to fold using the sequence model's own
overall confidence. Justified campaign-wide on 1,082 scored sequences,
rho = -0.299 against median fold error.

WHAT HAPPENED: on a single backbone at n=60 that signal is ABSENT.

LESSON: a between-group predictor used as a within-group ranker is a new,
untested claim. Measure it at the level you intend to use it.


=====================================================================
26. BELIEVING AN EXIT CODE, A MANIFEST RATE, OR A FILE SEARCH.
=====================================================================
Four separate instances, each of which read exactly like success:

  - rc=0 WITH NO OUTPUT. The predictor catches its own out-of-memory, skips the
    batch, writes no structure, exits 0. Reproduced deliberately on a probe: the
    heaviest glycan mode OOMed with rc=0 and ZERO structures written, while the
    lighter mode peaked at 94% of a 16 GB card and succeeded.
    ⛔⛔ AND THE OBVIOUS GUARD ALSO PASSES: the run's working directory came back
    FULLY POPULATED, so a file-EXISTENCE check reported success too. Only the
    output COUNT against the input count catches it.
  - COUNTING THE WRONG THING. Outputs live INSIDE the per-run archives, so
    searching for loose structure files returns 0 on a perfectly good round.
  - A MANIFEST'S OWN THROUGHPUT FIGURE. It divides cumulative outputs by the
    LAST attempt's elapsed time. 17% wrong on the shard we needed, and ~300x
    wrong on four other shards of the same campaign.
  - THE TOOL'S OWN ERROR TEXT. A fatal-error file blamed a missing MSA; the MSA
    was present and the cause was memory. A separate "ran out of memory" line in
    the same logs refers to the de novo binder chain's empty alignment and is
    EXPECTED, not a fault.

LESSON: verify the output COUNT per unit of work. Never rc, never a reported
rate, never the absence of loose files, and never the tool's own diagnosis.

AND ARCHIVE THE DIAGNOSTICS: five of six failed shards here kept a fatal-error
file and NO log, so their cause is not proven and never will be.


=====================================================================
27. LETTING RENTED WORKERS FINISH ON THEIR OWN. Four incidents.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENS: our workers do not self-terminate. On finish the container re-runs
its start command, the second run refuses, and it restart-loops at full billing
rate.

MEASURED INCIDENTS: ~8 workers idle 4.9 h with no terminator armed; five left
billing past a deadline; failed optimizer workers billing until caught by hand;
five that ran ~9.7 h on a 71-minute job after already writing a fatal error.

AND THE GUARD ITSELF FAILED TWICE, in different ways:
  - a process check for the terminator's own name MATCHES THE SHELL CARRYING THE
    PATTERN and reports healthy with zero terminators running. Measured against
    ground truth. The pattern must be anchored.
  - the terminator was ALIVE, blocked in a local copy on a full disk, logged
    nothing for 6 h 21 min, and every liveness check passed throughout.

LESSON: creating spend and ending it are two obligations. Arm the terminator
before you look away, verify it with a check that cannot match itself, and
measure whether it WORKS rather than whether it EXISTS. Treat termination as part
of the cost model — a published cost figure that assumes workers stop is wrong.

RELATED: never loop a create (a launcher holds shared state and is not
idempotent; a retry loop once nearly put a second worker onto the same output
prefix, which is the condition that lets one manifest bless two workers), and
never put two workers on one output prefix. A looping worker can also re-upload
over its own verified data until the remote copy stops matching its manifest,
leaving the local pull as the only good copy.


=====================================================================
28. QUOTING A LENGTH OUT OF THE MERGED PUBLIC TABLE.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENED: the prior-designs table's "length" column is the FULL construct —
binder plus a CONSTANT 621-residue target. Verified for all 400 rows. Rank
orderings are safe (Spearman between binder length and full length = 1.00); every
ABSOLUTE value is +621 too large. The median binder is 88.5 residues, not 709.

LESSON: check what a merged column actually measures before quoting an absolute
number from it. The rank-safe case is the dangerous one, because everything
downstream looks fine.


=====================================================================
29. LENGTH AS A WAY TO BUY INTERFACE. Measured: it buys helices.
=====================================================================
WHAT HAPPENED, measured on single-site designs: extra length buys HELICES, not
interface. Designs were 87% helix / 0% median strand and already spanned 37-57 A,
so additional length bought no reach.

LESSON WITH ITS OWN SCOPE LIMIT, recorded because it was nearly misapplied: that
measurement is about SINGLE-SITE binders. For a two-site bridge that must
physically cover a 45.6 A span, length IS load-bearing, and the single-site result
does not transfer. A measured rule carries the population it was measured on.


=====================================================================
30. SMALLER THINGS, EACH WITH A MEASUREMENT
=====================================================================
- A TEMPLATES BLOCK THAT IS SILENTLY IGNORED. The forced-template flag only takes
  effect when potentials are on, and the runner turns potentials on by GREPPING
  for a templates block. Without it the flag is silently ignored: measured target
  fit 17.5 A versus 0.69 A.
- A RELATIVE MSA PATH. Makes the predictor SKIP the design and exit 0. The
  builder now asserts a worker-absolute path rather than trusting it.
- THE PREDICTOR BUILDING THE WRONG RECEPTOR STATE. Measured on this target: the
  predictor builds the extended conformation, not the tethered one — a key
  distance came out 10.4 A against 29.0 A in the crystal. The assay target is
  tethered. Any prediction of a conformation-selective binder inherits that.
- THE PREDICTOR GETTING A SIDE CHAIN WRONG WHILE GETTING EVERYTHING ELSE RIGHT.
  On a refold of a known complex, fold and pocket were right and one rotamer was
  wrong, so the defining 2.84 A hydrogen bond was absent in 21 of 21 draws. A
  correct backbone is not a correct interaction.
- A SCRIPT GUARDED ON __main__ UNDER A HEADLESS VIEWER exits 0 writing nothing.
- A DIRECTORY LISTING COUNTING DIRECTORIES AS FILES, and a process-count pipeline
  counting its own shell. A count is not a measurement.
- A STRING-COMPARE WHERE A NUMERIC COMPARE WAS MEANT. Reads as a result.
- A REPORTED "RETRY OF MISSING SHARDS" STRING that appears on every round,
  because the test is "not yet landed" rather than "is a relaunch". It carries no
  information; it was chased once. Left unpatched deliberately — a live process
  shares the code path, and editing a running script does not fix it.
- A DUPLICATE PRE-LAUNCH LEDGER LINE per resume, because the ledger write runs
  unconditionally before the launch branch. Count distinct worker ids for spend,
  never ledger lines.
- A COLLISION THAT WOULD HAVE EXITED THE SCORER AFTER THE MONEY WAS SPENT: one
  substitution in a planned scan already existed as an earlier design. Caught
  before launch by hand AND against the scorer's own guard; excluded.

31. READING "SEVEN TIED LEADERS" AS A NOISE PROBLEM. They were one
    molecule, and one free measurement said so.
=====================================================================
TRIED: after six optimisation cycles, seven designs sat tied at the top of the
table with no statistical test separating any pair of them. The conclusion on
the record was "0 of 15 pairs among the top six separate" -- i.e. the metric is
too noisy to tell them apart. The implied next step was to replicate harder.

WHAT HAPPENED: all seven are the same length, so Hamming distance is exact and
costs nothing to compute. Measured 2026-10-06:

  the seven leaders differ from each other by 1-4 of 75 residues, mean 2.2
  the next two candidates were 25/75 and 17/75 away
  the eighth candidate was 4/75 away -- diversity exhausted after three

They are ONE MOLECULE with point mutations. Nothing separated them because
there was nothing there to separate.

WHY IT MATTERS, two ways:
  - The action is opposite. "Too noisy" says replicate more; "identical" says
    stop optimising this family and change premise. We would have spent another
    cycle on the first reading.
  - It changes what the tie means for a deposit. Submitting several members of
    a tied family buys no diversity, and we were about to treat seven leaders
    as seven options.

STATUS: fixed as a habit -- a Hamming (or equivalent distance) matrix over the
top candidates, before any claim about separability. It is free. The general
form is design_guidelines.txt J1 and J2.

=====================================================================
32. DIVIDING A REPRODUCIBILITY DELTA BY THE NEW ROUND'S STANDARD ERROR
    ALONE. Declared a valid round VOID, and punished the better round.
=====================================================================
TRIED: a decisive round carried an anchor sequence whose value from the
previous round was known, so that a batch shift could be detected. The test
written was: delta between the anchor's new and old values, divided by the new
round's standard error.

WHAT HAPPENED: the anchor came back +0.037. The new round had s.e. 0.010, so
that test read 3.55 s.e. and the code declared the round VOID -- discarding a
comparison in which the two challengers lost by 9.30 and 11.61 s.e.

The arithmetic is wrong. Comparing two ESTIMATES combines both standard errors:
sqrt(0.010^2 + 0.018^2) = 0.0210, giving 1.75 s.e. -- within tolerance, and the
round holds.

THE PERVERSE PART: the prior value's s.e. was 0.018 because that round was
heavily replicated (90 draws). Ignoring it means the BETTER-replicated the
previous round was, the more likely the new round is to be declared a batch
shift. The error punishes exactly the rounds that did the right thing.

CAUGHT BY: the verdict being implausible -- a round whose margins were 8-16x
the anchor's drift should not be void -- and then by checking the denominator.

STATUS: fixed. The corrected test is the committed one; the pre-fix version is
kept as a backup, and the superseded summary file is kept rather than
overwritten, because a retraction is a finding. General form: design_guidelines
J5.

=====================================================================
33. TAKING LOW LABEL VARIANCE AS EVIDENCE OF QUALITY. The most stable
    design in the cohort was the worst one.
=====================================================================
TRIED: among eight site-A designs, one was carried into the deposit partly
because it had the best label STABILITY of the eight (sd 0.038) with a mid-pack
average. Stability read as trustworthiness.

WHAT HAPPENED: three unrelated measurements then flagged that one design and
no other.

  axis                                      this design     the others
  strict fold-AND-full-footprint rate       0.178           0.80-0.86
  interface packing (LIS family)            0.000, 10/10    0.486 (best)
  backbone geometry vs the crystal target
    in the SAME file: tau ratio             4.29x           0.13x
    heavy-atom clash ratio                  1.98x           1.80x

It was the only candidate above its own in-file crystal control on BOTH
geometry measures. Its low variance was measuring that it sat CONSISTENTLY
near-but-not-at the thing we wanted.

AND IT MAKES THE MOST CONTACTS OF THE THREE -- mean 107, up to 172 in one
sample -- while scoring zero on packing, with the scorer confirming it FOUND
the interface and scored it zero. A high contact count with no packing is a
spread, non-specific interface, and a metric that COUNTS footprint contacts is
blind to it by construction.

HONEST LIMITS, because this is a flag and not a verdict: all three axes read
the same predicted structures, so they are not fully independent -- a
systematic predictor artefact would move them together. And the geometry ratio
rests on 10 models, where the best candidate has 70.

NOT RETRACTED, NOT DROPPED: the design is still in the deposit, as the only
real sequence diversity available (25 of 75 residues from the leader), with all
three flags stated in the accompanying documentation. That is a deliberate
diversity bet, not a co-equal pick, and it is labelled as one.

A SECOND DESIGN in the same deposit scores zero on packing but is BETTER than
its own crystal control on both geometry measures with zero buried unsatisfied
charges -- so its zero is a PLACEMENT risk, not a foldability one. Two zeros
with different causes; the geometry panel is what told them apart.

STATUS: the general forms are design_guidelines J3, J4 and J7. The specific
hole it exposes in our own pipeline: six cycles optimised a contact COUNT and
never once measured interface packing or expression-associated geometry.
