# Novelty measurement — index (2026-10-06)

Everything deposited here was measured on 2026-10-06 (NYC local) on local CPU, before the
challenge-1 submission deadline. Nothing here is a binding measurement, and **no number in this
directory is an affinity**.

## Start here

**`accidental_ctpr_homology.txt`** — the short note the rest of this material supports: while
checking our own designs' novelty before submitting, we found that our three 145 aa "bridge"
designs are close relatives of the published **consensus tetratricopeptide repeat (CTPR)**
proteins, which are themselves designed proteins. It was found by accident, it is reported
because it is useful to anyone running a similar pipeline, and the measured follow-up
(sequence novelty and structural novelty are separable) is in it.

## ⛔ Read the correction first

**`NOVELTY_CORRECTION_MONOMER_20261006.md`** — the structural-novelty numbers in the reports below
were first measured on **chain A extracted from a predicted complex**. The organizers fold the
submitted sequence **alone**. Re-measured on **693 predicted monomer structures**: **0 of 693 draws
reaches TM 0.80**, the highest single draw is **0.738**, the one design previously flagged as high
structural similarity (`egfr-siteA-id2015`, TM 0.885) is **not** — and **not one** nearest-neighbour
reference survives the change. The superseded numbers are left in place with banners, not deleted.
Raw tables: `data/siteA_monomer_20261006/`.

The scripts will not run unmodified from a clean checkout: they carry `/PATH/TO/...` placeholders where
machine-absolute paths were removed, and they read fold/database trees that are too large to
deposit. They are published so the method can be read and reimplemented, not as a turnkey pipeline.

⛔ **Two traps recorded in the scripts' own comments, because both silently produce wrong answers:**
1. MMseqs2 `-a` (backtrace) is **mandatory** — without it `fident`/`nident` are reported as
   `0.000`/`0` for every hit, at exit code 0, which reads as "0% identity to everything". Our first
   full run produced exactly that and was discarded.
2. Local vs global identity must be derived from the **local** alignment. Forcing a global
   Needleman-Wunsch alignment against a longer target inflates the figure — `id3017` vs `7udk_A`
   reads 30.7% one way and 40.0% the other.

## ⛔ THE COVERAGE LEVER WAS TESTED, AND IT IS A NEGATIVE RESULT

`BRIDGE_MUTANT_FOLD_RESULT_20261006.md` §3 identifies **coverage** as the only remaining lever: the
matched region is ~112 of 145 residues, i.e. 0.77 of the molecule, so adding roughly 16-20 residues
of non-matching structure would *arithmetically* push it below 0.70.

That was then measured — appending sequence to `id85` mutB and re-running the structural search over
504 structures, at four lengths. **It does not work, and the reason is instructive.**

⛔ **Observed, script not deposited.** The coverage-lever sweep below was run in the lab's own
working repository and neither the script nor its output table is part of this archive. The result
is reported because it is real and it changed what we did; it is **not reproducible from this
repository**.

| variant | length | TM, alignment-normalised | TM, query-normalised | TM, target-normalised |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V0 | 145 | **0.988** (42/42 ≥ 0.80) | 0.840 (42/42) | **0.944** (42/42) |
| V1, +20 | 165 | **0.989** (42/42) | 0.752 (**0/42**) | **0.944** (42/42) |
| V2, +30 | 175 | **0.992** (42/42) | 0.712 (**0/42**) | **0.943** (42/42) |
| V3, +57 | 202 | **0.991** (42/42) | 0.624 (**0/42**) | **0.942** (42/42) |

⛔⛔ **Only the query-normalised TM moves.** In the other two conventions nothing moves, and every
one of the 378 extended draws stays at or above 0.80.

⚠️ **On the counts in this section, stated plainly rather than left to be re-derived:** "504
structures" over four lengths and "378 extended draws" over the three extended lengths are both
**126 per variant**; the `42/42` in the table above is the per-variant, per-convention count
(42 × 3 conventions = 126). ⛔ **The run that produced them is not deposited** (see the note on
the coverage lever below), so an outside reader cannot recompute 126 from this archive and should
treat 504 and 378 as reported, not as verified here.

⇒ **The mechanism, which should have been stated before measuring:** appending residues cannot make
the matched core less similar to the reference — *it is the same matched core*. It only changes the
denominator. So "coverage is the lever" is **a statement about arithmetic, not about structure**, and
whether it buys anything depends entirely on a normalisation convention in the organizers' pipeline
that we have **not** verified. ⚠️ Our own earlier bridge figures (TM 0.967-0.977) sit in the
**alignment-normalised** range — i.e. most likely *not* the convention in which this gain exists.

⛔ Two further reasons it should not be relied on: a glycine/serine tail is disordered, and a
domain-segmentation step of the kind the organizers use can discard a disordered tail and score the
matched domain intact; and a ~20-57 aa appendage makes a new molecule with no binding evidence
behind it. **No coverage claim is made here.**

## ⚠️ These are our measurements, not the organizers' annotation

The competition's annotation pipeline runs **automatically after upload** and reports which
sequences passed its novelty filter. **No file in this directory states that outcome.** Everything
here is our own measurement, with our own tools, thresholds and databases, anticipating theirs.

⚠️ Two limits that make ours a **lower bound on similarity**, not a verdict: we searched **two** of
the five databases their annotation uses — patent sequence collections are the unsearched category
we would worry about first — and our TM convention and 0.80 threshold are ours rather than theirs.
