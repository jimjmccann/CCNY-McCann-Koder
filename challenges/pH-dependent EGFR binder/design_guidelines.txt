DESIGN GUIDELINES
Take-away messages for future de novo design projects.
McCann / Koder groups, The City College of New York.
Drawn from the 2026-09/10 EGFR binder campaign. Deliberately not EGFR-specific.

Every item below has a measurement behind it. Where the measurement is on one
campaign and one target, that is said. Nothing here is a preference dressed as a
rule; where we only have an argument, the item says "argument, not measurement".


=====================================================================
A. SAMPLING AND NOISE — get these right before anything else
=====================================================================

A1. MEASURE THE SAMPLE COUNT ON THE CONSTRUCT YOU WILL ACTUALLY FOLD. NEVER
    INHERIT IT.
    A draw/sample count that works on a small target does not transfer to a
    large one. Measured: 21 structure-prediction draws per sequence succeeded on
    100-180-token crops and had NEVER succeeded on the 696-token full construct.
    Launched anyway, five of six workers produced nothing in ~9.7 h on a 71-min
    job. The only count ever proven on the big construct was 3; 10 was an
    interpolation, and 10 was then separately proven (0 failures over 1,040
    structures) before being trusted.
    => Every campaign owes a one-line justification of its sample count against
    its own construct: token count, ligands, template, potentials.

A2. AN UNMEASURED SAMPLE COUNT GETS ONE CANARY SHARD FIRST.
    A failed sample consumes its full wall time and yields nothing, so a wrong
    count costs the whole arm rather than a fraction of it. A canary is minutes
    and a negligible fraction of the arm it protects.

A3. PRECISION HAS A CEILING SET BY HARDWARE, AND THE FIX IS REPEATS, NOT A
    BIGGER NUMBER.
    Measured: at 10 draws on a 75-residue binder the GPU sat at 97.6% memory.
    There was no headroom to raise draws. Precision then only comes from
    repeating the 10-draw measurement. Know which of the two levers you have
    before you plan the campaign.

A4. ESTABLISH A NOISE FLOOR BY RE-MEASURING, BEFORE YOU RANK ANYTHING.
    This campaign ran four cycles before anything was ever re-measured. Every
    design had exactly one measurement, so there was no noise estimate at all,
    and the two rows that looked like replicates were byte-identical values
    carried forward across three training tables.
    Measured when it was finally done: n=6 re-folds, mean |delta| 0.158 on a
    0-1 metric. The cycle's best new design beat its own parent by 0.009.
    => Without the floor that reads as progress. With it, it is nothing.

A5. RE-MEASUREMENT IS STANDING, NOT A ONE-OFF, AND IT RIDES INSIDE THE CYCLE.
    Refold the top ~5 of each cycle with fresh samples, in the cycle's own
    slots. That costs zero extra workers and controls for batch effects, because
    repeats and new designs are then measured in the same run. A separate fleet
    costs money and confounds batch with cycle.

A6. CARRY A KNOWN LOW ANCHOR, OR YOU CANNOT SEE SELECTION.
    Re-measuring only the champions tells you whether they hold up. It cannot
    tell you how much of the apparent gain was selection. Include one design
    whose prior value is known and LOW.
    Measured: over 6 repeats spanning the score range, slope of fresh-on-prior
    was +0.343. Both 1.000 champions came back 0.700 and 0.800; the low anchor
    came UP, 0.400 -> 0.600. That is regression to the mean, and it says part of
    every cycle's gain was selection, not improvement. A champions-only repeat
    round would have shown the fall and not explained it.

A7. SPANNING THE SCORE RANGE BUYS A CORRELATION; REPEATING ONE VALUE BUYS A
    YES/NO. Prefer the correlation. It is the same number of measurements.

A8. REPEATS ARE NOT NEW DATA POINTS. Never merge a repeat into the training
    table as a new row: it puts one design in twice, double-weights it in
    anything fitted on the table, and can corrupt reference selection. Score it,
    compare it to its prior value, keep it in a separate file.


=====================================================================
B. OBJECTIVES AND METRICS
=====================================================================

B1. A METRIC WITH A CEILING CANNOT RANK ITS OWN LEADERS.
    Measured: the contact metric had a hard ceiling of 11 (the footprint size).
    By cycle 1 the top three sat at identical means, dmean +0.00, Fisher
    p=0.628-1.000. The fallback fraction metric then also hit 1.000 on two
    designs. Saturation is foreseeable from the definition — check for it before
    you build the loop, not after it stops separating.

B2. PUT THE CONJUNCTION INSIDE THE SCORE. A FLOOR, NEVER A SUM.
    If you need two things in the same sample, score min(a, b), not a + b. A sum
    lets a near-best term cancel a near-worst one. A min is low if either side
    is weak, so it enforces the conjunction by construction.

B3. NEVER AVERAGE MARGINALS WHEN YOU NEED SIMULTANEITY.
    Measured: a design held every one of 11 required contacts in >=50% of its
    samples and held all 11 together in 1 sample in 10. On a two-site objective,
    one sequence scored 0.857 when each site was averaged separately — near the
    top — with ZERO samples that satisfied both sites at once; the per-sample min
    gave it 0.571. Kendall tau between the two rankings was +0.783, so this
    changes the order, not just the scale.
    => Average the per-sample conjunction. Do not conjoin the per-site averages.

B4. DO NOT SELECT THE SAMPLE ON THE QUANTITY YOU ARE AVERAGING.
    Measured: an objective that kept only samples passing a site-A bar and then
    graded site B deleted the low site-B measurements preferentially, because the
    two were correlated at +0.590 (p=1.9e-15): mean site-B contact was 4.15 in
    kept samples and 2.81 in dropped ones. One sequence's label rested on a
    single surviving sample and was inflated by +2.67; it ranked 1st and belonged
    outside the top 5. 10 of 36 labels rested on one sample.

B5. SCORING ZERO IS DIFFERENT FROM NOT REPORTING A SCORE.
    A sample that misfolded or docked wrong is still a measurement. Score it 0
    and keep it. The only admissible missing value is one where the predictor
    itself failed to produce a structure. Dropping "bad" samples is the same
    error as B4 wearing different clothes.

B6. A COUNT METRIC SATURATES AT THE SAMPLE COUNT. CHECK THE RESOLUTION
    ARITHMETIC BEFORE ADOPTING AN OBJECTIVE.
    Measured, 3 samples per sequence, 50 sequences: "count of samples satisfying
    both sites" took 1 distinct value (all zero). Relaxed, 4 values. A count over
    3 samples can take at most 4 values — a hard ceiling on resolution, not a
    tuning problem. The graded per-sample floor gave 33 distinct values over the
    same 50 sequences. Tabulate distinct values / zeros / median / max for every
    candidate objective on real data before choosing.

B7. A HARD REQUIREMENT DOES NOT NECESSARILY COLLAPSE RESOLUTION — MEASURE,
    DO NOT ASSUME.
    The expectation going in was that requiring one specific contact would zero
    ~36% of samples and destroy resolution. Measured over 98 sequences at 10
    samples: 31 distinct values and NO zeros at all, against the previous
    metric's 11. Because the term is averaged over enough samples, a sequence
    almost never misses in all of them. The first recommendation was reversed by
    the measurement.
    => And the scope is the sample count: this property depends on averaging over
    enough samples. At 3 samples the same arm showed 21 of 50 sequences at
    exactly 0. Do not carry the result across sample counts.

B8. LABEL THE METRIC ON EVERY NUMBER YOU QUOTE.
    Two scorers coexisted here for a day. Kendall tau between them was 0.707 and
    one design sat at rank 63 under the old metric and rank 7 under the new one.
    An unlabelled score is unusable six weeks later and dangerous the same week.

B9. CHANGING THE OBJECTIVE RENUMBERS THE HISTORY. Say so, and do not retro-score
    a landed table silently. Every table scored before an objective change was
    scored on the old one; that is a fact about the table, not a defect.

B10. WATCH THE TRUE CRITERION; REGRESS ON THE TRACTABLE ONE. KEEP THEM APART.
    Where the thing you actually want is too rare to optimise (here: samples
    satisfying both sites at once, single digits in hundreds), optimise the
    graded proxy and WATCH the rare criterion as a separate column. Never
    regress on it and never quietly swap one for the other.
    And know they can disagree: measured, the graded label and the true criterion
    picked DIFFERENT backbones — one scored 0.464 vs 0.195 on the label
    (p=7.2e-06) and had never once satisfied both sites in 150 samples, while the
    one that had was 8/109 vs 0/804 across the rest (Fisher p=3.3e-08). That
    choice was made by a human, on the record, and not by the label.


=====================================================================
C. SURROGATE MODELS AND THE OPTIMIZATION LOOP
=====================================================================

C1. A SURROGATE ENTERS THE LOOP ONLY AFTER IT IS SHOWN TO BEAT PREDICTING THE
    MEAN. STATE THE GATE AS A NUMBER BEFORE YOU FIT IT.
    Gate used here: out-of-bag RMSE / sd(y) < 1.0, and quote it with its standard
    error — a ratio of 0.999 printed as "1.00" was once read as a pass, and the
    verdict flipped on the third decimal. Require ratio + 1 s.e. < 1.0.
    Measured: 1.14 and 1.04 on the two site-A labels — at or worse than
    predicting the mean. Trained on the saturated label it predicted 11.696 and
    12.101 against a physical ceiling of 11.
    => The model was taken OUT of the loop and the cycle ran exhaustive
    enumeration instead. Exhaustive coverage needs no ranking, so nothing was
    lost.

C2. "UCB BEAT RANDOM" IS A MEASUREMENT, NOT AN ASSUMPTION.
    Measured: +0.394 +/- 0.634, t=+0.62, Fisher p=1.000. No skill. An
    acquisition function on a model with no skill is a random sampler with
    overheads.

C3. MORE SEQUENCES IS NOT ALWAYS THE FIX. MEASURE THE LEARNING CURVE BEFORE
    BUYING n.
    Measured by subsampling, 3 repeats per size: OOB ratio 1.077 / 1.031 / 1.064
    / 1.077 / 1.028 at n = 20/30/40/50/60. FLAT across a 3x range, and the n=60
    value sat inside n=30's scatter. The apparent gain from pooling an earlier
    campaign was noise.
    => The honest reading was that the label at 3 samples carried too little
    information PER SEQUENCE for the map to be learnable at any reachable n. The
    fix was more samples per sequence, not more sequences. An earlier conclusion
    of "n is the constraint" was itself withdrawn by this curve.

C4. ASK WHETHER THE LABEL IS LEARNABLE AT ALL, FOR FREE, BEFORE PAYING FOR THE
    LOOP.
    A simple ridge + random-forest floor on data already held costs nothing and
    answers "is there signal a simple model can find". A negative there is the
    informative result: the loop would stall at cycle 1 and you would learn it
    after paying.
    Caveat recorded with it: held-out burn-in sequences sat ~21 mutations from
    the training set while the optimizer's proposals sit 1 mutation from a parent
    with data nearby. Local ranking can be easier than the global test. Do not
    read a weak global r as "the loop cannot rank single mutants".

C5. CHECK ZERO-INFLATION EACH CYCLE BEFORE TRUSTING ANY MODEL RANKING.
    Measured: 21 of 50 sequences at exactly 0 on one backbone, 7 of 50 on
    another. With a fifth of the table at one value, the OOB number is the only
    thing standing between you and a confident ranking of noise.

C6. SIGNAL AT ONE LEVEL IS NOT SIGNAL AT ANOTHER.
    Measured: sequence-model confidence correlated with fold quality campaign-wide
    across 1,082 sequences (rho = -0.299) and was ABSENT within a single backbone
    at n=60. A between-group predictor used as a within-group ranker is a new,
    untested claim.

C7. ENUMERATION ENDS. PLAN FOR IT.
    Measured: the combination walk over the accepted set completed at 88
    combinations (50 + 38, no order 7) and the last cycle beat nothing — the
    driver's own line was "cycle sequences beating the previous best: 0". When
    enumeration is exhausted the proposer has no derivable content and the next
    cycle is a science decision, not a scheduling one.

C8. A REFUSAL IS A RESULT. Build the proposer to refuse — on a thin reference
    margin, a reference that fails the quality gate, an empty accepted set, a
    fully-enumerated set — rather than to guess. A wrong reference silently
    redefines every mutation in the cycle.

C9. CHOOSE THE REFERENCE BY A MEASURABLE RULE, NOT BY JUDGEMENT.
    Rule used: the training row with the most Hamming-1 neighbours (the "hub"),
    because the accepted set is only meaningful relative to the sequence whose
    single mutants were actually measured. Measured margin: 50 neighbours against
    4 for the runner-up — not a tie and not a judgement call. With a thin margin
    the script stops.

C10. APPLY EVERY QUALITY FLOOR TO THE REFERENCE AS WELL AS TO THE CANDIDATES.
     Measured: one design topped the table on 3 of 10 passing samples with a
     median fold error of 1.871 A. Anchoring on a thin-data outlier flatters
     everything downstream.

C11. NEVER RE-FOLD WHAT IS ALREADY IN THE TABLE, EXCEPT AS A DECLARED REPEAT.
     It costs compute to learn nothing, and it is indistinguishable from the
     repeats in A5 unless the repeats are declared in their own file. Identify
     repeats from that file only — never by id prefix. "id9" as a prefix already
     matched ten genuine designs here.

C12. SLOW PROGRESS IS NOT EVIDENCE THE ARM IS DEAD, AND IT IS NOT EVIDENCE IT IS
     ALIVE EITHER. Decide that on the noise floor (A4) and the aggregate test —
     measured here as between-design variance 3.02x and 2.42x the binomial null
     (chi2 p=2.5e-11, p=1.3e-06). The aggregate signal was real while no single
     design was resolvable. Those are two different questions and they had two
     different answers.


=====================================================================
D. STRUCTURE PREDICTION AS A SCORER
=====================================================================

D1. GATE ON FOLD QUALITY BEFORE RANKING ON CONTACTS. THIS IS THE SINGLE MOST
    EXPENSIVE LESSON HERE.
    A contact-only ranking of backbones named six winners. FOUR OF THE SIX FAILED
    the fold gate. The top pick had a median Ca fold error of 8.45 A — the
    predictor had not built the designed shape at all, so every one of its 11
    "full-footprint" samples was contact against a different structure.
    Arm-wide the gate removed 53% of full-footprint contact: 422 samples -> 200.
    31 of 118 backbones failed the median gate and 16 of those 31 had >=5
    full-footprint samples — the contact metric was actively promoting
    unbuildable backbones.
    => Order is: did it build the designed shape, then did it dock where
    designed, then how good is the interface.

D2. KEEP FOLD FIDELITY AND PLACEMENT FIDELITY AS TWO NUMBERS.
    sc_rmsd (binder superposed on binder alone) = did it build the shape.
    pose_rmsd (superpose on the target, then measure the binder with no
    re-alignment) = did it dock where designed. pose >= sc always.
    sc small + pose large = folded right, docked wrong. sc large = nothing else
    means anything. One combined number hides which failure you have.

D3. CUT ON MEDIANS PER GROUP, NEVER ON COUNTS.
    A truncated or uneven sample set reads too high. This has already reversed a
    winner on this project. Pool every sample of a group, ASSERT the expected
    total, and refuse if it is short.

D4. NEVER RANK ON PARTIAL DATA. Groups span shards; a group scored on the
    shards that happened to land reads too high. Assert the total.

D5. VERIFY OUTPUT COUNTS. NEVER AN EXIT CODE.
    Measured repeatedly: the predictor catches its own out-of-memory condition,
    skips the batch, writes no structure and returns SUCCESS. rc=0 with zero
    structures is a normal, silent outcome. The only proof a round is complete is
    counting the outputs — and counting them inside the archives, because
    searching for loose files returns zero on a perfectly good round.
    The failure is also non-deterministic: a re-run of the same shard reported
    zero failures. One worker of six surviving is not evidence the config works.

D6. BUILD THE EXCLUSION CHECK, AND BUILD IT SO IT CAN FAIL FROM INSIDE THE
    PIPELINE. (See E1.) Where the scorer and the generator share a definition,
    COPY it rather than re-derive it, and say in the file that a change to one
    must change the other. Two independent derivations of "the same" geometry
    disagree silently.

D7. A PREDICTOR THAT REFUSES YOUR INPUT MAY BE RIGHT. One scorer refused these
    folds with "target residues differ: design 231 vs pred 621". It was correct:
    the integer overlap was a coincidence, and relaxing the count check would
    have superposed mismatched residues and returned a confident, meaningless
    number. A second scorer was written rather than the check loosened.

D8. SELF-CONSISTENCY IS A CHECK ON THE PREDICTOR, NOT JUST ON THE DESIGN.
    Prediction and design are different models. Measure design-vs-prediction
    agreement explicitly; do not assume the prediction reproduces the backbone
    you asked for.


=====================================================================
E. TARGET PREPARATION
=====================================================================

E1. VALIDATE A TRUNCATED TARGET BY EXCLUSION, NOT BY INCLUSION. THIS VOIDED A
    WHOLE ROUND.
    The target crop was validated as "all 11 footprint residues are present" and
    never as "nothing that matters is absent". Measured afterwards: 70% of
    site-A and 100% of site-B designs sat within 2 A of receptor that had been
    deleted — backbone overlap, not contact. Worked example: one receptor residue
    sat 0.66-1.01 A from binder backbone atoms. No rotamer relieves that.
    Everything from the generation stage onward was voided.
    Three compounding causes, all worth naming:
      - the crop was CHANGED to recover three footprint residues at one end; it
        was a SHIFT, not an extension, and silently deleted 60 residues at the
        other end;
      - the exclusion WAS noticed two days later and filed under a heading about
        numbering being verified — the tick went to the arithmetic, the residue's
        absence was the evidence rather than the subject;
      - every downstream stage treated the crop AS the target, so no script in
        the pipeline could possibly have detected the omission.
    => A check that cannot fail is not a check. Write the one that superposes the
    design back onto the WHOLE target and measures the part the generation target
    did not contain.

E2. A SPHERE BEATS A WINDOW, AND CONTIGUITY WAS NEVER THE REQUIREMENT.
    Measured: the generator holds a non-contiguous target rigid — 21 scattered
    segments kept their mutual geometry to 0.0402 A, 978/978 atoms out, nothing
    invented in the gaps. So cut a sphere around the site and keep far more real
    target than any contiguous window of the same size.

E3. TRIMMING PRESERVES COORDINATES, NOT PHYSICS. A residue whose neighbours were
    cut is artificially exposed. The radius is a guess; the exclusion gate of E1
    is the only thing that proves the guess was generous enough.

E4. THE GATE MUST SUPERPOSE. Measured: the generator recenters its output by
    66.93 A, the same offset on every model. A gate that diffs raw coordinates
    calls every design a catastrophic clash; one that passes everything is
    equally wrong. And because the target comes back rigid at ~0.04 A, the
    superposition RMSD is itself a tripwire — assert it.

E5. WHERE A CUT IS MULTI-SEGMENT, NO CONSTANT OFFSET EXISTS. PERSIST THE MAP.
    Measured: three separate scorers hardcoded an offset valid only for the old
    contiguous window. On the 15-segment sphere one of them mapped the key
    histidine to a cysteine — it failed loudly. Another's offset "worked" by luck
    because all of its footprint residues happened to fall in the first segment.
    => Persist the map at cut time (the builder originally computed it and threw
    it away), ship it with the target, and have every consumer load it. Verify it
    against real generator output by RESIDUE TYPE, not by count: an anchor-only
    check cannot catch a wrong map.

E6. ASSERT RESIDUE IDENTITY, NEVER A NUMBER RANGE. A range check is the wrong
    test the moment the design target and the assay target are numbered
    differently, and the two numbering schemes will overlap by coincidence.

E7. NAME THE CHAIN, EVERY TIME. Chain conventions differ between generator
    versions (binder = A here, the opposite in the predecessor). Chain selection
    has silently changed a conclusion on this project more than once.

E8. READ THE TARGET OUT OF THE ASSAY DATA RATHER THAN CHOOSING IT. Here the
    assay construct was read from the organiser's own table — binder plus a
    constant 621-residue target — so no design was forced to dock at a face we
    had picked. That also means a design's apparent epitope is a measurement, not
    an assumption.

E9. AN ABSOLUTE LENGTH QUOTED FROM A MERGED TABLE IS SUSPECT. Measured: a
    public table's "length" column was the FULL construct, binder plus the
    constant 621-residue target. Rank orderings were safe (Spearman 1.00);
    every absolute value was +621 too large. The median binder was 88.5
    residues, not 709.


=====================================================================
F. GENERATION AND CONDITIONING
=====================================================================

F1. READ WHAT THE GENERATOR WAS TRAINED TO DO, IN ITS INSTALLED SOURCE, NOT IN
    ITS DOCUMENTATION.
    Measured by reading the installed package: the hotspot channel had ~53%
    training share; the H-bond donor/acceptor channels ~14-20%; the
    buried/exposed channels ~0% for the protein-only case — and that channel
    dies in a transform that early-returns without a ligand, on both pipeline
    branches. So "keep this residue at the rim" was not sayable at all. A
    separate claim that a particular external tool was required turned out to be
    wrong and had suppressed the one channel the design depended on.
    => Conditioning you cannot express is not a design decision; it is a filter
    requirement. Find that out by reading the code, before the campaign.

F2. THE GENERATOR CAN BE TOLD WHAT TO TOUCH. IT CANNOT BE TOLD WHAT TO AVOID.
    There is no exclusion or anti-hotspot conditioning in the current generator;
    the previous version had it and it is gone. Everything expressed as "avoid"
    lives in the filter. => Generate wide, filter hard, and recognise that the
    filter is where the design actually happens — it is not QC.

F3. A SPREAD CONSTRAINT SET MAY BE UNSATISFIABLE. TEST THE MINIMAL ASK AS ITS
    OWN ARM.
    Measured: orientation is inferred from the hotspot CENTROID. One requested
    hotspot 12.2 A from the centroid of the others was reached by 0 of 336
    designs, with r(distance from that hotspot, contact rate) = +0.967.
    => Run the everything arm and the minimal-anchors arm together. If the
    everything arm returns nothing and the minimal one returns designs, the
    spread is the problem and you learned it for one shard instead of a campaign.

F4. EXPENSIVE SAMPLER KNOBS GET REFUTED, NOT ASSUMED. Measured on this
    generator: classifier-free guidance 2.76x the null, recycling 2.55x
    null-to-worse. Both were turned OFF for production after measurement, having
    been turned on for a reason that sounded good.

F5. THE PARAMETER THAT MATTERS MAY NOT BE WHERE YOU THINK. Measured: binder
    length lives in the contig string's leading integer, and the schema forbids
    extra keys — so a `length` key alongside it is either silently ignored or
    fails at load. Write the one the tool reads, and have the script refuse if it
    cannot find it.

F6. FIX ONE VARIABLE PER SHARD WHERE IT IS FREE. One fixed length per shard made
    "is longer more novel" readable off the output archive names with no extra
    bookkeeping. Design the sharding so a question you will ask later is free.

F7. CHECK WHETHER INFERENCE IS SEEDED BEFORE PAYING FOR REPLICATE SHARDS.
    Verified in source here: the seed defaults to None and is only written into
    output metadata; seeding appears only in training code. If it had been
    seeded, three shards at one length would have been byte-identical and we
    would have paid 3x for one shard.

F8. REQUEST A PROPERTY AT THE GENERATION STEP RATHER THAN FILTERING FOR IT —
    BUT SET THE MAGNITUDE FROM A BATCH, NOT FROM THE PILOT. THIS ONE REVERSED.
    Measured on n=8: the generator's own sequence head produced near-neutral
    binders and a charge requirement culled 5 of 8 designs, all five on that one
    bound. At a handful of designs ultimately tested, discarding ~62% of the pool
    for a property you could request is unaffordable. So the decision was "bias
    it in", with the MAGNITUDE deliberately left to a tuning batch.
    MEASURED AT n=3,320, THE DIRECTION WAS WRONG. The pool already overshoots
    on that property about 6:1 — in-band 77.6% and 74.6% at the two sites, with
    44 vs 283 and 138 vs 335 failing on the two opposite bounds. A bias would
    have pushed MORE sequences out the far side of the band. The bias shipped at
    ZERO.
    => Two lessons, and the second is the bigger one. (a) Measure-then-commit
    earned its keep: a magnitude set from n=8 would have hurt. (b) A decision can
    be RIGHT about which property to control and WRONG about which way, and only
    the batch measurement separates those. An n=8 pilot can establish that a
    property needs attention; it cannot establish the sign.

F9. CLOSE THE KNOWN SEQUENCE LIABILITIES FOR FREE.
    Omit cysteine unless you need it: measured on an earlier arm of this lab,
    permitting it gave 2-3 cysteines per design in a protein whose wild type has
    zero, which never paired (partners 12-15 A apart) — free thiols, an
    oxidation and aggregation liability with no compensating structure. It
    confounded the design pool and the question had to be deferred by decision
    rather than resolved.
    Grep the N-glycosylation sequon (N-X-[ST], X != P) in the same pass. Same
    cost, not covered by omitting cysteine. Both are routine practice at
    frontier labs.
    => And note the inverse error was live in this same repository at the same
    time: one arm omitted proline and permitted cysteine. Both settings existed
    here and only one was right for a single-chain binder. Audit your own flags.

F10. ALLOW PROLINE WHERE CAPS AND TURNS ARE LOAD-BEARING. If the design needs
     free backbone NH and C=O to complement target polar groups, those exist only
     at helix caps and in loops — mid-helix amides are consumed by the helix's own
     i->i+4 bonds. Proline is the capping residue; omitting it forces caps out of
     residues that cap badly and blurs exactly the boundary the requirement needs
     to be crisp. Not a licence to scatter prolines: the fold-back check is what
     catches misplacement.

F11. PIN WHAT MUST NOT MOVE, AND FIND IT PER STRUCTURE.
     The sequence model will happily redesign a deliberately placed residue —
     nothing in the output marks it. Locate it per design by its own contact
     geometry and pass it as fixed. Measured: the generator chose the pinned
     residue's sequence position itself, 68 distinct positions over a 2-70 window
     in an n=400 baseline, so the index cannot be hardcoded.
     And keep the target in the input as context while designing only the binder
     chain, so the model sees the interface it is designing against.

F12. DO NOT IMPORT A SCREEN FROM A DIFFERENT PROBLEM.
     A sphericity screen was built for a project that needed a globular protein
     to hold a cofactor in an interior. A binder has the opposite requirement: it
     must present a complementary face. A highly spherical binder is if anything
     worse — it minimises contact area per residue. The rest of that project's
     tooling did not transfer either: one piece was a control experiment with no
     pre-registered gate, another was descriptive rather than thresholded, and a
     third depended on a broken system library.
     => Ask what the borrowed metric was FOR before reusing it.

F13. THE FILTER SPLITS WHERE ITS INPUTS DO. Net charge and surface-patch metrics
     need a SEQUENCE, so they cannot run on backbones. Backbone-level: hotspot
     engagement, interface contact and burial against the target, secondary
     structure, radius of gyration. Sequence-level, after the sequence model:
     charge bounds, largest positive patch, alphabet compliance, sequon grep.
     Patch metrics needing per-atom solvent accessibility run after the
     fold-back, not before.


=====================================================================
G. STATISTICS AND HOW TO KNOW A RESULT IS REAL
=====================================================================

G1. PRE-REGISTER THE THRESHOLD THAT WOULD RETRACT THE FINDING, IN WRITING,
    BEFORE THE NUMBER EXISTS.
    Done once here and it paid for itself: a decoy control was specified in
    writing with its retracting thresholds before it was run. They were met, and
    the correction took two hours instead of becoming a premise in the next three
    documents.

G2. A SINGLE-CONDITION AUROC CANNOT DISTINGUISH "OUR TARGET SITE IS RIGHT" FROM
    "THIS DESIGN STICKS TO ANYTHING". SHIP A DECOY ARM.
    Measured: the site-A claim scored AUROC 0.633 (CI excluding 0.5, p=0.0055)
    and beat all 21 confidence metrics. The same 402 designs folded against a
    control face nobody had targeted scored 0.656 — higher. Paired difference
    A - B = -0.022, 95% CI [-0.135, +0.094]. The epitope-specific interpretation
    was retracted; the AUROC itself still reproduces byte-for-byte. What was
    withdrawn was what it MEANT.

G3. STRATIFY BEFORE QUOTING A POOLED STATISTIC.
    Same data, stratified: the pool was a mixture of 99 ligand-mimic designs
    (binding rate 29.3%) and 149 genuinely designed interfaces (11.4%). The
    mimics engaged the control face MORE and the real site LESS, so pooling
    inflated the control and suppressed the real site — Simpson's paradox. In the
    designed stratum the real site survived a length control (0.715, p=0.0045)
    and the control face did not (0.557, p=0.45).
    => But the direct contrast STILL failed: paired A - B = +0.087, 95% CI
    [-0.097, +0.264]. "A passes its own test and B fails its own" is WEAKER than
    "A beats B". Two underpowered tests with opposite verdicts are not a measured
    difference. The original decisions did not move.
    => Standing rule earned: quote that class of metric stratified or not at all.

G4. DO NOT OPTIMISE A METRIC THAT CORRELATES WITH A VARIABLE YOU SET AT DESIGN
    TIME.
    Measured: binder length alone scored AUROC 0.575 and correlated with the
    engagement metric (+0.249, +0.379). Length is ours to choose, so optimising
    engagement directly would partly optimise "make it longer". That is Goodhart,
    not affinity. Regress the design-time variable out, or keep the metric as a
    rank and not an objective.

G5. KEEP A REFUTED METRIC UNDER A WEAKER NAME IF IT STILL RANKS. The engagement
    metric was kept as "docks tightly to the face it is handed" — still the best
    measured screen available, with a CI excluding 0.5 in both arms. It ranks; it
    sets no absolute threshold and it is not an objective. Demotion is cheaper
    and more honest than deletion.

G6. A RETRACTION IS A FINDING. Keep the banner in the file's own bytes, never in
    version-control metadata, and never remove it. Metadata self-invalidates the
    moment a later edit moves the date.

G7. CORRECT THE DOCUMENT IN PLACE WITH A NAMED ADDENDUM. Two documents here were
    corrected by their own authors; both name the sentence they correct. That is
    what makes the correction survive being read by someone else.

G8. DISTINGUISH A RESIDUE THAT IS NECESSARY FROM ONE THAT IS RATE-LIMITING.
    Measured: one footprint residue was contacted in 63.8% of samples against
    98.8% for the strongest — weakest of the 11 by ~13 points, and it was the
    single missing residue in 89.8% of samples that missed the ceiling by exactly
    one. That makes it the RATE-LIMITING contact. All eleven are necessary; one
    governs the score.
    The rate did not move when the campaign explored a different mutation order,
    which makes it ARCHITECTURAL rather than a property of the substitutions
    tried — and the accepted set the optimizer had been walking contained no
    position that reaches it. That is why three cycles of enumeration beat
    nothing.
    => Measure which component is limiting before optimising. "Recover one
    specific contact" is a targetable objective; "the fraction is below 1" is not.

G9. A CAUSAL TEST BEATS A CORRELATION, AND A NEGATIVE ONE STILL COUNTS.
    Measured: substituting the one position that governs the limiting contact
    took the score 0.40 -> 0.00 while FOLDING BETTER than its parent — so the
    position is causal. 18 of 19 substitutions there were unfolded.
    => But governing a quantity is not the same as being improvable. A saturation
    scan at that position found nothing above the noise floor (best 0.609 vs
    parent 0.600, floor 0.158). The identity at that position was not the lever;
    the pose was. Run the mechanism experiment, and accept a null from it.

G10. A HEADLINE NUMBER CAN BE AN ARTIFACT OF HOW IT WAS DIVIDED. Measured: a
     throughput figure in a run manifest divided cumulative outputs by the LAST
     attempt's elapsed time — 17% wrong here and ~300x wrong on four other
     shards of the same campaign. Fit rates from clean, same-shape points you
     have checked, and say which points.

G11. READ THE SERIES, NOT THE AVERAGE. A reported value is not a measurement.


=====================================================================
H. MONEY, COMPUTE AND SEQUENCING THE WORK
=====================================================================

H1. BUY THE CHEAPEST MEASUREMENT THAT CAN REFUTE THE PLAN, FIRST.
    Examples from this record, all real:
      - a 32-minute, 3-arm generator run, specified in advance with the gate that
        would drop the primary generator entirely and fall back to an
        alternative. Designed to be refutable in 32 minutes.
      - a learnability floor on data already held, no compute, before any worker (C4).
      - the exclusion gate (E1), local CPU, which would have saved an entire
        voided round.
      - a local re-analysis of structures already on disk that answered a
        mechanism question with no new compute and reframed the next cycle.
    => Rank candidate experiments by (chance it changes the decision) / cost. The
    cheap refutation usually wins by orders of magnitude.

H2. A DECISION WORTH MAKING IS WORTH MAKING REFUTABLE. Write the gate — the
    number and the direction — before the run. "If arm C is indistinguishable
    from arm A, the channel is decorative and we drop the generator" is a usable
    sentence. "We'll see how it looks" is not.

H3. SPEND BOUNDS ARE ARBITRARY; RE-DERIVE THEM, DO NOT INHERIT THEM. One
    per-shard time bound here existed to cap the unprotected work and was
    raised when the real risk was computed — explicitly because the original was
    arbitrary. A bound that cannot be re-derived from a cost is a superstition.

H4. REAP WHAT YOU LAUNCH, AND TREAT IT AS PART OF THE COST MODEL.
    Rented workers do not stop themselves: on finish the container re-runs its
    start command, fails, and restart-loops at full rate. Measured incidents
    here: ~8 workers idle 4.9 h; five left running past a deadline; five that ran
    ~9.7 h on a 71-minute job after already writing a fatal error.
    => Pair every launch with a verifying terminator armed BEFORE you look away,
    and verify the terminator is alive with a check that cannot match itself —
    a process grep for its own pattern matches the shell carrying the pattern and
    reports healthy with nothing running. Anchor the pattern.
    And measure whether the watcher WORKS, not whether it EXISTS: one here was
    alive, blocked on a full disk, logged nothing for 6 h 21 min, and every
    liveness check passed.

H5. ONE UNIT OF WORK PER WORKER, ONE OUTPUT PREFIX PER WORKER. Two workers
    sharing an output prefix means the first to finish writes a complete-looking
    manifest and the verifier can bless or kill the wrong one.

H6. A LOOPING WORKER CAN DESTROY ITS OWN VERIFIED DATA by re-uploading over it
    until the remote copy no longer matches its manifest. The local pull may be
    the only good copy. Verify, pull, THEN terminate.

H7. NEVER LOOP A CREATE. Capacity retries go one at a time with a health check
    between; a launcher holding shared state is not idempotent.

H8. ARCHIVE THE DIAGNOSTICS, NOT JUST THE OUTPUTS. Measured: five of six failed
    shards here carried a fatal-error file and NO log, so their cause is not
    proven and never will be. Only the sixth is evidence. The per-run archive
    must include the log.

H9. A TOOL'S OWN ERROR MESSAGE CAN BE WRONG. Measured: a fatal-error file blamed
    a missing input file; the input was present and the real cause was memory.
    Believing it would have sent the next person to fix the wrong thing.

H10. PRECISION BUYS WHAT IT BUYS AND NO MORE. Measured: 10 samples gives
     +/-15 percentage points on one sequence's rate — enough to separate a >50%
     sequence from a ~33% pack, NOT enough to separate 43% from 33%. Downstream
     ranking must respect that. And it buys precision on SEQUENCES only: within-
     backbone sequence variance was 10x the between-backbone variance here, so no
     sample count on one backbone settles the backbone question.

H11. A MODEL SIZE LIMIT MAY BE A TRAINING LIMIT, NOT A MEMORY LIMIT. Measured:
     the 384-token ceiling here is the training crop. A bigger card buys
     memory, not validity. Check which kind of limit you are lifting.


=====================================================================
I. PIPELINE HYGIENE THAT TURNED OUT TO BE SCIENCE
=====================================================================

I1. REFUSE RATHER THAN DEGRADE. Every gate in this pipeline that saved something
    was a refusal: a scorer that exits if a required column is absent rather than
    inferring it; a builder that asserts its skeleton rather than trusting it; a
    proposer that stops on a thin margin. Silent defaults are how a stale table
    gets published at rc=0.

I2. CHANGE EXACTLY ONE LINE OF A WORKING CONSTRUCT.
    The fold construct here — target chain, 10 glycans, glycan bond constraints,
    a forced template, a worker-absolute MSA path — was discovered by measurement
    and the script that built it was not kept. Rewriting it from scratch is the
    expensive way to make a silent mistake. The replacement takes a REAL landed
    input as a skeleton and changes only the binder sequence line, carrying every
    other detail byte-for-byte. It then ASSERTS the skeleton: one binder sequence
    line, a worker-absolute MSA path (a relative one makes the predictor skip the
    design and exit 0), and a templates block (without it the forced-template
    flag is silently ignored — measured, target fit 17.5 A vs 0.69 A).
    => And PROVE the new inputs differ from the old ones in exactly the intended
    way. Done here for a 50-sequence round: every file differed from a real
    landed one in exactly the chain-A sequence line, 50 of 50, because a rebuilt
    construct would have silently broken the pooled comparison.

I3. COPY, DO NOT EDIT, A RUNNER THAT ANOTHER LIVE CAMPAIGN DEPENDS ON. And list
    the differences in the copy's header.

I4. DO NOT EDIT A RUNNING SCRIPT AND EXPECT THE FIX TO BE LIVE. It is not live
    until the process restarts. Note the defect, say it is unpatched, and say
    why.

I5. PARAMETERISE, DO NOT FORK, WHEN A SECOND CAMPAIGN NEEDS THE SAME MACHINERY.
    A forked copy drifts invisibly; a parameter shows up in a diff.

I6. A SCRIPT THAT APPENDS TO ITS OUTPUT WILL MERGE TWO CAMPAIGNS SILENTLY.
    Fresh output path per experiment, with no error and no symptom until an
    analysis averages across both.

I7. MAKE THE STEP ORDER EXPLICIT AND MAKE IT IDEMPOTENT. The order matters here
    (D1), and idempotency is what let a hand-built cycle be picked up by the
    automatic driver without re-deriving it.

I8. WRITE DOWN WHY, NOT JUST WHAT. Every threshold in this pipeline that
    survived has its provenance in the file that uses it. Every one that caused
    trouble was a number someone inherited. If you cannot say where a constant
    came from, that is the finding — record it as unknown rather than inventing
    a rationale for it.

I9. VOIDED DATA MAY STILL ANSWER METHOD QUESTIONS. Test: is the defect in the
    causal path of the QUESTION? "Is design X a binder?" — no, the design was
    built into deleted target. "Does score P predict score Q within a backbone?"
    — yes, both were computed on the same coordinates and the defect does not
    enter the correlation. If you read voided data, mark every number in the same
    sentence as the number, never plot it on the same axes as good data, and
    write nothing back into the quarantine.


=====================================================================
J. CHOOSING WHAT TO DEPOSIT — added 2026-10-06, from the submission pass
=====================================================================

J1. BEFORE CONCLUDING YOUR METRIC IS TOO NOISY TO SEPARATE YOUR LEADERS,
    MEASURE HOW DIFFERENT THE LEADERS ARE.
    Seven designs sat tied at the top of a table and no test separated any
    pair of them. The reading we nearly took was "the metric is too noisy".
    Measured instead: the seven differ by 1-4 of 75 residues, mean 2.2. They
    are ONE MOLECULE with point mutations. There was nothing there to
    separate.
    => "No separation" has two causes and they call for opposite actions.
    Noise says replicate more. Identity says stop optimising and go somewhere
    else. One Hamming-distance matrix, costing nothing, tells you which.
    => And it changes what a tie MEANS downstream: submitting several members
    of a tied family buys no diversity at all.

J2. MEASURE DIVERSITY BY FARTHEST-POINT SELECTION, NOT BY COUNTING LEADERS.
    "We have ten top designs" is not ten molecules. Seed at the best, then
    repeatedly add whichever candidate is farthest from everything chosen so
    far, and watch the minimum distance. Measured here: 25/75, then 17/75,
    then 4/75 -- the drop to 4 is where diversity ends, and it ended after
    three. Every further slot bought <= 4 residues of difference.
    => Report the minimum-distance sequence, not the count. It makes the
    exhaustion visible instead of arguable.

J3. SELECT ON AT LEAST ONE AXIS THAT IS NOT YOUR OPTIMISATION OBJECTIVE,
    AND RUN IT BEFORE YOU COMMIT, NOT AFTER.
    We optimised footprint contact and fold self-consistency for six cycles.
    On the day of deposit we ran two panels that had never been run on these
    sequences -- an interface-packing panel and a post-fold geometry panel --
    and they flagged TWO of the four candidates, one of them on three
    unrelated measurements. Both panels were local, took seconds, and needed
    no new compute.
    => An objective you have optimised against is the axis your designs are
    least informative on. The cheap orthogonal check is worth more at the end
    of a campaign than at the start, and we ran it at the end by accident
    rather than by plan.
    => Caveat it honestly: if every axis reads the same predicted structures,
    they are not fully independent. A systematic predictor artefact moves all
    of them together.

J4. LOW VARIANCE IS NOT QUALITY. IT IS CONSISTENCY, AND THE TWO COME APART.
    One design had the BEST label stability of its cohort (sd 0.038) and a
    mid-pack average, and was then flagged by three other measurements: the
    worst strict pass rate of the eight (0.178 against 0.80-0.86), zero
    interface packing in 10 of 10 samples, and the worst backbone geometry of
    any candidate. It was consistently near-but-not-at the thing we wanted.
    => A stability statistic answers "will this number repeat", never "is this
    number good". Never let it stand in for the second question.

J5. COMPARING TWO ESTIMATES USES BOTH STANDARD ERRORS.
    An anchor carried from a previous round came back +0.037. Divided by the
    NEW round's s.e. alone that is 3.55 s.e. and the round is void; combined
    properly, sqrt(0.010^2 + 0.018^2) = 0.0210, it is 1.75 s.e. and the round
    holds. The prior value is itself an estimate with uncertainty, and
    ignoring that makes any WELL-REPLICATED new round look like a batch shift
    -- i.e. the error punishes exactly the rounds that did the right thing.
    => Any time you test "did this reproduce", check whether the denominator
    contains both uncertainties. This one inverted a verdict.

J6. CARRY MORE THAN ONE ANCHOR IF THE ROUND HAS TO DECIDE SOMETHING.
    One anchor cannot distinguish a batch shift from a real change in that one
    sequence. Two can, because they can move in opposite directions. We used
    two in one round and one in the next, and the second round's control is
    weaker by construction. It only failed to matter because the margins being
    judged were 8-16x larger than the anchor's own drift.

J7. A LARGE CONTACT COUNT WITH NO INTERFACE PACKING IS A BAD SIGN, NOT A
    GOOD ONE.
    One design made the MOST contacts of its cohort -- mean 107, up to 172 in
    one sample -- and scored exactly zero on every packing metric, in all 10
    samples, with the scorer reporting that it FOUND the interface and scored
    it zero. That is what a spread, non-specific interface looks like.
    => A metric that COUNTS contacts is blind to this by construction. If
    counting is your objective, you need a packing measure beside it.

J8. REPORT A GEOMETRY OR QUALITY METRIC AS A RATIO TO A CONTROL IN THE SAME
    FILE, SCORED BY THE SAME CODE.
    We had no calibrated threshold for backbone geometry or clash density, and
    inventing one is how a screen quietly becomes a fake gate. What we had was
    a crystal-derived target chain sitting in every model file. Scoring the
    design AND that target with the same code gives "normal for this code, at
    this resolution, with these conventions", and the ratio is readable where
    the absolute is not.
    => Every predicted design beat its own crystal control on Ramachandran
    (0.00-0.45x), which is expected and is exactly why the ratio and not the
    absolute is the quantity to quote.
    => Say what the metric is NOT. Ours was not a MolProbity clashscore and
    not a MolProbity Ramachandran test -- the hydrogens and contour grids were
    unavailable -- so it was named as a heavy-atom clash count and a coarse
    favoured-region test, with both definitions written into the script.

J9. MEASURE WHAT THE ASSAY GATES ON FIRST, NOT WHAT YOU FIND MOST
    INTERESTING.
    Our competition assays EXPRESSION before affinity: a design that does not
    express scores nothing. Six cycles of optimisation measured contact and
    fold, and nothing in the programme had ever measured an
    expression-associated property -- buried unsatisfied charges, exposed
    apolar surface, backbone strain -- until the day of deposit.
    => Order your screens by the order of the gates you will actually be
    judged at.

J10. NORMALISE BEFORE COMPARING DESIGNS OF DIFFERENT LENGTH.
    A 145-residue design and a 75-residue design cannot be compared on counts
    or total surface area. Raw totals made the longer design look markedly
    worse on exposed apolar area; per residue it was 29% above the shorter one,
    which is a small difference, and it was perfect on backbone geometry.
    => Per-residue or per-100-residue, every time, and state the lengths.

J11. A SCREEN THAT DISCARDS IS NOT A SCREEN THAT RANKS, AND ITS OWN
     DOCUMENTATION USUALLY SAYS WHICH.
     The interface panel we used states in its own output that it discards the
     clearly-bad and does not rank, that published thresholds must not be
     lifted because each was calibrated on a different predictor, that one of
     its metrics points BACKWARDS on the relevant benchmark, and that this
     particular target sits near the bottom of fifteen for filterability --
     expect 3-6x enrichment, not separation.
     => Read the tool's own warnings before its numbers, and quote the
     warnings alongside the numbers. On our cohort one of its metrics did not
     discriminate at all; quoting that one in support of a pick would have
     been selecting the metric that disagreed.

J12. WHEN A DEPOSIT'S ANNOTATION AND ITS DOCUMENTATION DISAGREE, THE
     DOCUMENTATION IS NOT ENOUGH.
     Our sequence file's description line for one design recorded the single
     flag known when the file was written. Two further flags on the same
     design were measured hours later and live only in the accompanying
     document. A reader who parses the file and not the prose gets the
     out-of-date story.
     => Either regenerate the annotation, or say in the document, at the point
     of the claim, that the file's own header is incomplete. Do not assume the
     prose will be read.

=====================================================================
WHAT IS NOT SETTLED
=====================================================================

- No design from this campaign has been tested experimentally. Every number
  above is computational, on one target, with one generator and one predictor.
  The assay-side replicate scatter we have a figure for is 2.8x at the 90th
  percentile, which no computational precision here approaches.
- The objective in force at the end of the campaign had not been applied to any
  landed table; the tables were scored on its predecessor.
- The closed loop demonstrably works as machinery and has not yet been shown to
  produce a better binder: the aggregate progression is real, no individual
  design is resolvable above the noise floor, and part of the apparent gain is
  measured selection.

Honest framing for any write-up: this is a working closed-loop optimizer with a
measured progression and a measured noise floor. It is not a finished binder.
